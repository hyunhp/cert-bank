@ Q004210 explain
G: ## Costs that scale with object count
S3 bills some things per object, not per GB:
- **Lifecycle transition requests**: each object moved to a colder class is one request, with Deep Archive among the highest rates.
- **Archive metadata overhead**: every object in Glacier Flexible Retrieval or Deep Archive carries about 32 KB billed at the archive rate plus 8 KB billed at the Standard rate.
- **Minimum billable size**: Standard-IA, One Zone-IA and Glacier Instant Retrieval bill at least 128 KB per object.
- **Minimum storage duration**: Standard-IA and One Zone-IA charge for at least 30 days; an object moved out earlier is billed for the rest of the 30 days.
## Worked example
50 million 8 KB objects are about 400 GB of data, but in Deep Archive they add about 2 TB of metadata overhead every month (50 million x 40 KB), plus 50 million transition requests.
Combining each hour's logs into one `.tar.gz` of a few hundred MB turns that into a few dozen objects per day.
Note that since September 2024, lifecycle rules by default do not transition objects smaller than 128 KB, because moving tiny objects one by one rarely pays off. A rule like the one in this scenario moves 8 KB logs only because an object size filter (`ObjectSizeGreaterThan`) overrides that default.
W: Aggregate before archiving: Deep Archive charges a per-object transition request and adds about 40 KB of metadata overhead to every object, which is five times the size of each log. Packing thousands of logs into one compressed object removes almost all of those per-object charges and also shrinks the stored data.
X: B=Glacier Flexible Retrieval instead: it still charges per-object transitions and the same per-object metadata overhead, and its storage price is higher than Deep Archive for 5 years of data.
C=Standard-IA first: this doubles the transition requests per object. Standard-IA also bills each 8 KB log as 128 KB, and it has a 30-day minimum storage duration: moving objects on to Deep Archive 23 days later adds a pro-rated charge for the rest of those 30 days (and a single lifecycle rule cannot even schedule the second transition before the minimum duration has passed).
D=Intelligent-Tiering: objects under 128 KB are never monitored or moved to cheaper tiers and always stay in the Frequent Access tier, so archive tiers never apply to them. For 8 KB logs it saves nothing.
T: For millions of tiny objects, per-object request fees and minimum sizes dominate, so aggregate them into larger objects before archiving instead of just picking a colder class.
