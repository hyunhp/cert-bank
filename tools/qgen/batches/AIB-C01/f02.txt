S: A fraud-detection model's accuracy has declined significantly since deployment, and investigation shows that the distribution of transaction features in production now differs substantially from the training data distribution. This is an example of which reliability risk?
A: Data drift, a common cause of model decay
B: An explainability gap in the model's outputs
C: A fairness violation across customer groups
D: A privacy breach involving training records
K: A
G: ## Data drift and model drift
A model learns the patterns in its training data. After deployment the world keeps changing.
- **Data drift**: the distribution of input features changes, for example average transaction amounts rise or new merchant types appear.
- **Concept drift**: the relationship between inputs and the correct answer changes, for example fraudsters adopt new tactics.
- **Model drift** (performance decline) is the result: accuracy drops because the model faces data unlike what it learned from.
## How to detect it
Capture a baseline of feature statistics from training data, then compare production inputs against it on a schedule. Amazon SageMaker Model Monitor does this with data quality monitoring and raises alerts when statistics move beyond thresholds. The usual fix is retraining on recent data.
W: Data drift: production feature distributions have moved away from the training distribution, which is the textbook cause of the accuracy decline described.
X: B=Explainability gap: explainability is about understanding why a model made a prediction; the issue here is that predictions got worse because inputs changed.
C=Fairness violation across groups: fairness concerns unequal outcomes across groups; nothing in the scenario points to one group being treated differently.
D=Privacy breach of training records: a privacy breach is exposure of personal data; changing input distributions and falling accuracy do not involve data exposure.
T: Accuracy falling because production inputs no longer look like training data is data drift; detect it by comparing against a training baseline and retrain.

S: Which statement best compares a chatbot, an assistant grounded in company data, and an agent?
A: A chatbot converses; a grounded assistant cites company sources; an agent can also plan and act
B: The three share the same capabilities and differ mainly in what vendors charge for them
C: A chatbot plans and acts through tools, while an agent answers from general knowledge

K: A
G: ## Three levels of AI assistants
- **Chatbot**: holds a conversation using the model's general knowledge or scripted flows. Example: answers "What are your opening hours?" from a fixed script.
- **Grounded assistant**: retrieves company sources (policies, manuals, product data) and answers from them, often with citations. Example: "What is our refund policy for damaged items?" answered from the current policy document.
- **Agent**: can also plan multiple steps and take actions through tools or APIs. Example: checks an order, confirms eligibility and starts the refund.
## Why the difference matters
Each level adds capability and risk. Agents can change records, so they need permissions, limits and human approval for sensitive actions. On AWS, Amazon Bedrock Knowledge Bases supports grounded answers, and Amazon Bedrock AgentCore supports building new tool-using agents (Amazon Bedrock Agents remains available to existing customers).
Note: Amazon Bedrock Agents was renamed Bedrock Agents Classic and closed to new customers on 2026-07-30; existing customers can keep using it, and AWS points new agent builds to Amazon Bedrock AgentCore. Exam questions may still name Bedrock Agents.
W: Chatbot converses, grounded assistant cites company sources, agent also plans and acts: this matches the increasing capability from conversation, to answers grounded in company data, to planning and executing actions.
X: B=Same capabilities, different price: the three differ in what they can do (use company data, call tools, take actions), which changes their value, risk and required controls.
C=Chatbot acts, agent only answers: this reverses the roles. Taking actions through tools is what defines an agent, while a basic chatbot only converses.
D=Grounded assistant uses general knowledge: being grounded means answering from company sources. A plain chatbot is the one that usually relies on general knowledge.
T: Chatbot, grounded assistant and agent add capabilities in order: conversation, answers from company data, then planning and actions.
