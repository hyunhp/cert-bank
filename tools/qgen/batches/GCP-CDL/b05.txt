@ GCP-CDL D3 T3.1 applied definition
S: A hospital network's operations director receives a weekly dashboard showing admissions, average stay and bed occupancy by site. She asks a colleague which term describes the practice of turning this historical data into charts and reports that managers read to monitor the business. Which term is correct?
A: Business intelligence, which presents historical and current data in dashboards and reports to support monitoring and decisions
B: Generative AI, which creates new text, images or code in response to prompts written by the managers who read the reports
C: Machine learning training, which adjusts a model's internal parameters until it predicts bed occupancy without any reports
D: Data ingestion, which moves records from source systems into storage but does not present or analyze them for managers
K: A
G: ## Data analytics versus business intelligence
**Data analytics** is the broad practice of examining data to find patterns, explain what happened and support decisions. It ranges from simple counts to statistics and machine learning.
**Business intelligence (BI)** is the part of analytics that makes data easy for people to consume: dashboards, reports and visualizations built on trusted, governed data. Typical BI questions are "how many?", "how are we doing against target?" and "which site is behind?".
## How the neighbours differ
- **Machine learning** learns patterns from data to predict or classify; BI shows what already happened.
- **Generative AI** produces new content; a dashboard summarizes existing numbers.
- **Data ingestion** is only the movement of data into a platform and is a step before analysis.
On Google Cloud, Looker is the business-level BI product, built on top of data in BigQuery.
W: Business intelligence: weekly dashboards and reports of admissions, stays and occupancy are the standard definition of BI, which turns data into a view managers can read.
X: B=Generative AI: it generates new content from prompts, whereas the dashboard only reports numbers that already exist.
C=ML training: training builds a predictive model from data; it is not the practice of presenting historical figures to managers.
D=Data ingestion: ingestion only moves data into storage; nothing in it presents or interprets the data for a manager.
T: BI = dashboards and reports on historical data for monitoring; analytics is the wider practice, ML predicts, gen AI creates.
V: Looker, BigQuery

@ GCP-CDL D3 T3.1 applied comparison
S: A retailer has two requests. Team X wants a score for each customer showing the likelihood that the customer will stop buying in the next quarter, based on past behavior. Team Y wants first drafts of personalised marketing emails for each customer segment. Which pairing of AI approaches fits?
A: Team X needs generative AI to write the score, and Team Y needs a rule-based system with fixed email templates
B: Team X needs predictive machine learning trained on past behavior, and Team Y needs generative AI that creates new text
C: Team X needs a business intelligence dashboard to predict churn, and Team Y needs predictive ML to draft the emails
D: Team X and Team Y both need predictive ML, because generative AI cannot produce text for marketing purposes
K: B
G: ## Predictive ML versus generative AI
Both are machine learning, but they answer different business questions.
- **Predictive (discriminative) ML** learns from labelled history and outputs a prediction: a number, a probability or a category. Churn likelihood, fraud score and demand forecast are examples.
- **Generative AI** uses foundation models to create new content such as text, images, code or summaries from a prompt.
## Choosing
Ask what the output is. A score about future behavior points to predictive ML trained on past customer data. A draft email is new content, which is the strength of generative AI.
A dashboard (BI) reports what happened; it does not forecast an individual customer's behavior by itself.
W: Predictive ML for churn and generative AI for email drafts: the first output is a likelihood learned from history, and the second is newly created text.
X: A=Gen AI for the score, rules for emails: this swaps the strengths, since a score is a prediction and fixed templates cannot produce fresh drafts per segment.
C=BI for churn, predictive ML for emails: a dashboard shows past results and does not predict individuals, and predictive ML outputs scores or labels, not drafted text.
D=Predictive ML for both: generative AI is built to create text, so the claim that it cannot write marketing copy is wrong.
T: Predict a future outcome from history = predictive ML; create new content = generative AI.

S: A telecom provider introduces AI agents in customer support. The agents resolve many billing and plan-change requests end to end by using internal systems. @ GCP-CDL D3 T3.1 applied tradeoff

@ GCP-CDL D3 T3.1 applied troubleshooting
S: A regional airline's AI team notices that many records in its customer data lack a phone number or email address. The marketing team wants to use the data to train a model that predicts which passengers will respond to a campaign. Which data-quality dimension is the main problem?
A: Completeness, because required information is missing from many records
B: Timeliness, because the records were not updated within the expected time window
C: Uniqueness, because the same passenger appears more than once in the table
D: Validity, because the values present do not follow the required format or range
K: A
G: ## The six dimensions of data quality
Data used for AI must be fit for its purpose. Google's data quality documentation describes these dimensions:
- **Completeness**: the data contains all the information required for its intended purpose.
- **Uniqueness**: values are distinct, with no duplicates.
- **Timeliness** (also called freshness): the data is recent enough to be useful.
- **Validity**: values conform to the required format, range or other standard.
- **Accuracy**: values are correct and reflect reality.
- **Consistency**: the same value is the same across tables and systems.
## Applying it
Missing phone numbers and emails are information that is absent. Nothing says the existing values are wrong, duplicated, stale or oddly formatted, so completeness is the dimension violated.
W: Completeness: required contact details are absent in many records, so the data lacks information the campaign model needs.
X: B=Timeliness: this concerns how recent the data is, and nothing in the stem says the records are out of date.
C=Uniqueness: this concerns duplicate records, but the problem is empty fields, not repeated passengers.
D=Validity: it applies to values present that break a format or range; here the values are simply missing.
T: Missing required values = completeness; duplicates = uniqueness; stale = timeliness; wrong format or range = validity; incorrect = accuracy; disagreeing = consistency.

@ GCP-CDL D3 T3.1 applied comparison
S: A product-catalog table lists one SKU's colour as "Teal", which is an allowed value in the colour field and is spelled and formatted correctly. The physical product is actually orange, and customers return it. Which statement about this data is correct?
