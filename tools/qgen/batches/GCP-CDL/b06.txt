@ GCP-CDL D3 T3.2 applied tradeoff
S: A regional insurer must add automatic transcription of recorded phone claims to its claims portal before the busy season starts in six weeks. Transcription is a common task, the insurer has no machine learning staff, and the transcripts do not need to be a source of competitive advantage. Which approach best fits these strategic factors?
A: Call a pre-trained speech API such as Speech-to-Text, which delivers the capability quickly with little development effort or ML expertise
B: Train a custom speech model from scratch on the insurer's recordings, because only a self-built model can ever be considered accurate
C: Hire a research team to design a new model architecture first, then decide later whether the portal needs transcription at all
D: Wait for the busy season to end and then fund a multi-year programme that builds a general-purpose speech platform in-house
K: A
G: ## Strategic factors when choosing an AI approach
A leader weighs a few factors before picking how to get an AI capability:
- **Speed**: how soon the business needs it.
- **Development effort**: how much building, data preparation and maintenance the team must do.
- **Differentiation**: whether the capability is a source of competitive advantage or a commodity.
- **Technical expertise**: whether the organization has people who can build and run models.
- **Choice and flexibility**: whether the approach lets the business change models or tools later.
## The spectrum on Google Cloud
Ready-made, pre-trained APIs (Vision, Translation, Speech-to-Text) are fastest and need the least expertise. Foundation models such as Gemini can be prompted or tuned. AutoML and custom training on Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform (formerly Vertex AI) give more differentiation at the cost of more data, time and skill.
## Reading the scenario
Short deadline, no ML staff, common task, no differentiation: every factor points to the pre-trained API end of the spectrum.
W: Pre-trained speech API: transcription is a standard task that Speech-to-Text already does, so the insurer meets the six-week date without ML staff or training data.
X: B=Custom model from scratch: it maximizes effort and time for a task that is not a differentiator, and a pre-trained API is not inherently less accurate.
C=Research team first: designing a new architecture is the slowest, most expertise-heavy path and ignores the deadline.
D=Multi-year in-house platform: it misses the busy season and invests heavily in a commodity capability.
T: Common task plus tight deadline plus no ML skills = start with a pre-trained API.
V: Speech-to-Text

@ GCP-CDL D3 T3.2 applied tradeoff
S: A specialty lender holds fifteen years of its own repayment histories and believes that a model trained on this data would predict defaults better than any competitor's generic tool. Leadership accepts a longer project to gain an advantage that rivals cannot copy. Which factor most strongly argues for a custom model on Agent Platform rather than a ready-made API?
A: Differentiation, because a model trained on proprietary data can capture patterns that a generic pre-trained service cannot offer to competitors
B: Speed, because training a custom model always finishes faster than calling a service that already exists and is ready to use
C: Reduced expertise, because custom training on proprietary data removes any need for data preparation or for evaluating results
D: Uniformity, because every competitor using the same generic service would then get identical predictions, which the lender prefers because it makes outcomes easy to compare

G: ## Differentiation is the reason to build
A pre-trained API is trained by Google on broad data and is available to everyone, so it cannot encode what is unique to your business. When the capability is the product or the edge, the unique asset is your own data, and a model trained or tuned on it can produce results competitors cannot buy.
## What you pay for it
Custom models cost more time, data preparation and skill than calling an API. That is why the choice is a trade-off: build when differentiation justifies the effort, buy when the task is common.
## Reading the scenario
Fifteen years of proprietary history and a willingness to wait make differentiation the deciding factor.
W: Differentiation: a model trained on the lender's own repayment history can learn patterns specific to its customers that no shared, generic service contains.
X: B=Speed: custom training takes longer than calling an existing API; the lender is accepting that cost, not avoiding it.
C=Less expertise: custom work needs data preparation and evaluation; lower expertise is a reason to prefer ready-made services or AutoML.
D=Uniformity: sameness of output is the problem a differentiating model avoids, not a benefit the lender wants.
T: Proprietary data and a need to stand out favour custom models; speed and low effort favour ready-made services.
