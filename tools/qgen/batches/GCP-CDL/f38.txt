S: A company's staff use a generative AI tool to draft customer replies. One reply confidently described a refund policy that does not exist. Leaders want to keep using the tool. Which action best addresses this business risk?
A: Have people review drafts before sending, and ground the tool in real company policies
B: Trust the drafts as written, since a fluent and confident reply is usually a sign that it is correct

D: Withdraw the tool from customer replies and go back to staff writing each reply by hand
K: A
G: ## Hallucinations and their business implications
Generative AI creates new content (text, images, code) by predicting likely outputs. It can produce fluent text that is **wrong** (a *hallucination*). The business implications are accuracy, trust, legal exposure and brand risk.
Practical mitigations:
- **Human review** of outputs for customer-facing or high-impact content.
- **Grounding** the model in trusted company data, so answers draw on real policies.
- Clear guidelines and monitoring for responsible use.
## Reading the scenario

W: Review and grounding: people check drafts before customers see them, and grounding the tool in the actual policies makes its content more reliable while the productivity gain is kept.
X: B=Trusting fluent drafts: confident, well-written answers can still be false, as the invented refund policy shows; fluency says nothing about accuracy.
C=One-sentence replies: a short reply can state a non-existent policy just as easily as a long one; length does not decide accuracy.
D=Withdrawing the tool: leaders want to keep the productivity gain, and manageable controls such as review and grounding address the risk without giving it up.
T: Manage gen AI hallucination risk with human review and grounding in trusted company data, not by trusting fluency, shortening output or abandoning the tool.

B=At rest: stored data stays encrypted on disk while queries run; the CISO already covered this state.
C=In transit: the stem states that connections to the service are encrypted, so that state is covered.
T: At rest and in transit are commonly encrypted; data in use is the state confidential computing protects.
