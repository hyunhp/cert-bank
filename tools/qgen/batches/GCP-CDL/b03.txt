@ GCP-CDL D2 T2.1 applied use-case
S: A grocery chain wants to stop discovering empty shelves only when customers complain. Leaders want store managers to see sales and stock movements as they happen so replenishment can be ordered the same day. Which business value of data does this goal illustrate?
A: Generating real-time business insights, so that managers can act on events while they are still happening
B: Archiving records for later audits, so that the chain can prove what was sold once each year has closed
C: Lowering hardware spending, so that the chain can retire a few older servers in its head-office building
D: Standardizing store layouts, so that every outlet displays the same products in the same positions
K: A
G: ## Why data is valuable to an organization
The exam guide lists several ways data creates value: generating real-time business insights, identifying trends, informing strategic decision making and fueling AI. Data on its own is just stored records; it becomes valuable when it changes what people decide or do.
## Real-time insight versus later reporting
- **Real-time insight**: events such as a sale or a stock movement are analyzed as they arrive, so a person or system can act the same day or the same minute.
- **Historical reporting**: the same records are reviewed afterwards to explain what happened last quarter.
A grocery chain that wants to replenish shelves the same day needs the first kind. The goal is faster action, not storage, savings or store design.
W: Real-time business insights: seeing sales and stock as they happen lets managers order replenishment before shelves stay empty, which is exactly the value the leaders describe.
X: B=Audit archive: keeping records for auditors is a legitimate use of data, but it looks backwards and does nothing to prevent today's empty shelves.
C=Hardware savings: retiring servers is an infrastructure cost topic and says nothing about using sales and stock data to act faster.
D=Store layout standardization: consistent layouts may help branding, but the scenario is about timely information, not about how products are arranged.
T: One core value of data is real-time insight: acting on events while they are still happening.

@ GCP-CDL D2 T2.1 applied use-case
S: An insurer's executives are surprised that a pilot AI claims assistant gives vague and sometimes wrong answers. The assistant was built quickly, but the claims records it learns from are scattered across departments, partly outdated and inconsistently labeled. Which statement best explains the situation in terms of the role of data?
A: Data fuels AI, so the quality, completeness and accessibility of the data behind a model strongly shape how good its results are
B: AI replaces the need for data, so the answers should have been correct even though the records were scattered and outdated

G: ## Data as the fuel for AI
The exam guide names "fueling AI" as one of the reasons data is valuable. Models learn patterns from data and, in generative and agentic applications, are grounded in an organization's own records. If those records are scattered, stale or inconsistent, the model inherits the same problems.
## What a leader should take away
- AI projects are data projects first: finding, cleaning and governing data usually decides success.
- More data is not automatically better. Relevant, current and trustworthy data is.
- Fixing the data foundation often gives a larger gain than changing the AI tool.

@ GCP-CDL D2 T2.1 applied comparison
S: A retail bank runs an application where customers transfer money. Each transfer must be recorded immediately and correctly, and thousands of such small updates happen every minute. Separately, the finance team wants to analyze years of combined data from many systems for reports. Which pairing matches these two needs?
A: A database for the transfers, and a data warehouse for the cross-system reporting
B: A data warehouse for the transfers, and a database for the cross-system reporting
C: A data lake for the transfers, and a database for the cross-system reporting
D: A database for the transfers, and a second copy of that same database for the cross-system reporting
K: A
G: ## Database versus data warehouse
- **Database**: stores the data an application needs to run. It handles many small reads and writes, such as recording a single transfer, and keeps each record accurate at that moment. This is often called operational or transactional work.
- **Data warehouse**: an enterprise platform for analysis and reporting of structured and semi-structured data from many sources, such as point-of-sale, marketing and CRM systems. It keeps current and historical data in one place for a long-range view over time. BigQuery is Google Cloud's serverless data warehouse.
## Why they are kept separate
Heavy reporting queries over years of data compete with the quick updates that customers are waiting for. A warehouse gets a copy of the data, shaped for analysis, so reports do not slow the application.
W: Database for transfers, warehouse for reporting: the database serves the running application with fast, accurate updates, and the warehouse consolidates many systems for analysis over time.
X: B=Warehouse for transfers: warehouses are built for analysis and reporting, not for recording thousands of individual customer updates.
C=Lake for transfers: a lake stores raw data at scale for analytics and ML; it is not the system that records each transaction for an application.
D=Second copy of the same database: a replica of an operational database is not designed to combine many source systems or to give a long-range analytical view.
T: Databases run the application; data warehouses combine data from many sources for analysis and reporting.
V: BigQuery, Cloud SQL

@ GCP-CDL D2 T2.1 applied use-case
S: A streaming company collects clickstream logs, video thumbnails, support-call recordings and sensor files. Its data scientists do not yet know what questions they will ask, and they want the original raw data kept as it arrived so they can experiment with machine learning later. Which approach fits best?
A: A data lake, which stores large amounts of data of all types in its native format without a predefined schema
B: A transactional database with a fixed schema, so that every file is converted into rows before it is stored
C: A traditional on-premises warehouse that accepts only cleaned and structured tables from a nightly batch load
D: A spreadsheet shared by the data scientists, where each file type is summarized into columns by hand
K: A
G: ## What a data lake is
Google Cloud describes a data lake as a centralized, scalable and secure repository that stores large amounts of structured, semi-structured and unstructured data in its native format. Without a predefined schema, teams can ingest any data at any speed and keep the "full-fidelity" original context that data scientists need for experiments and AI.
## When it is the right choice
- The questions are not known yet, so data cannot be shaped in advance.
- The data is mixed: logs, images, audio, documents.
- Machine learning needs the raw detail that cleaning would remove.
Cloud Storage commonly serves as the storage layer of a lake on Google Cloud.
W: Data lake: it keeps all data types in their native format with no fixed schema, so scientists can return to the raw data for later experiments.
X: B=Fixed-schema database: forcing audio, images and logs into rows loses the raw detail and the flexibility the team wants.
C=Traditional warehouse with nightly batch: it accepts only cleaned structured data, so the raw files the scientists want would be discarded or excluded.
D=Shared spreadsheet: summarizing files by hand cannot hold the volume and does not preserve the original data.
T: A data lake holds any data in its raw, native format, which suits exploratory analytics and machine learning.
V: Cloud Storage

@ GCP-CDL D2 T2.1 applied definition
S: A furniture retailer wants to personalize its newsletter using the purchase history, website browsing behavior and loyalty-program sign-ups that it collects directly from its own customers. How is this data classified?
A: First-party data, because the retailer collected it itself from its own customers and channels
B: Second-party data, because it is about customers who each belong to a different retailer
C: Third-party data, because it describes people and so must come from an outside data aggregator
D: Public data, because anyone browsing the retailer's website could in principle see all of it

G: ## Who collected the data?
The three source categories in the exam guide are defined by relationship to the organization:
- **First-party data**: collected directly by the organization from its own customers and channels, such as purchases, site behavior, app usage and loyalty sign-ups.
- **Second-party data**: another organization's first-party data that it shares directly with you, usually under an agreement, for example from a partner.
- **Third-party data**: data collected by an outside party with no direct relationship to your customers and sold or licensed broadly, for example by data aggregators.
These are generally accepted definitions; Google Cloud product pages do not give one canonical wording.
## Applying it
The retailer owns the relationship and collected the records itself, so it is first-party data. It is typically the most relevant and trusted of the three.
W: First-party data: the retailer gathered the purchases, browsing and sign-ups directly from its own customers, which is the definition.
X: B=Second-party: that label applies when a partner shares its own first-party data with you; here the retailer is the original collector.
C=Third-party: data about people is not automatically third-party; it depends on who collected it, and here the retailer did.
D=Public data: browsing behavior and purchase history are private records of the business, not open information anyone can see.
T: First-party data is collected directly by the organization from its own customers.

- The sharing arrangement should respect consent, such as the travelers' opt-in.
- It differs from third-party data, which is bought from a broad market with no direct partnership.

W: Second-party data: the airline collected it first-hand and shared it directly with a partner under agreement, which makes it second-party for the hotel group.

@ GCP-CDL D2 T2.1 applied definition
S: A logistics company receives shipment events as JSON messages. Each message has labeled fields such as "shipmentId" and "status", but different carriers add different extra fields, and there is no single fixed table design. How is this data best described?
A: Semi-structured data, because it has labels and organization but does not follow a rigid, fixed table schema
B: Structured data, because a labeled field in a message proves that the data belongs in fixed rows and columns
C: Unstructured data, because anything that arrives as text messages carries no recognizable organization
D: Unlabeled data, because the extra carrier fields mean that nobody tagged the data with the correct outcome
K: A
G: ## Three data shapes
- **Structured data**: fits a fixed schema of rows and columns, such as a transaction table.
- **Semi-structured data**: has tags, keys or other markers that give it organization, but no rigid table schema. JSON, XML and many log formats are examples. Records can differ from one another.
- **Unstructured data**: has no predefined model, such as free-text documents, images, audio and video.
## Applying it
JSON shipment events carry field names (organization) but vary between carriers (flexible schema). That is the semi-structured case. Data warehouses and lakes on Google Cloud can both hold this type.
## A common mix-up
"Unlabeled" is a machine learning term about whether examples carry a known outcome. It is unrelated to the data's structure.
W: Semi-structured data: labeled JSON fields give it organization, while differences between carriers show there is no rigid fixed schema.
X: B=Structured: fixed rows and columns need a consistent schema, which varying carrier fields do not provide.
C=Unstructured: text-based messages with keys and fields still carry organization, unlike free text, images or audio.
D=Unlabeled: that term refers to missing outcome tags used for training, not to the shape of the data.
T: Semi-structured data has labels or tags but no rigid table schema; JSON and logs are typical.

@ GCP-CDL D2 T2.1 applied use-case
S: A city transport authority wants to map its data supply chain. It starts with the moment a passenger taps a card on a bus reader, which creates a travel record that did not exist before. Which stage of the data supply chain is this?
A: Data genesis, the point at which data is first created or generated by an event, device or interaction
B: Data processing, the point at which raw records are cleaned and transformed into a more useful form
C: Data analysis, the point at which people or systems examine data to find patterns and answers
D: Data activation, the point at which insights are put to work in an action, product or process
K: A
G: ## The data supply chain
A data supply chain follows data from creation to business use. The exam guide names six stages:
1. **Genesis**: data is created by an event, such as a card tap, a purchase or a sensor reading.
2. **Collection**: data is gathered and brought in from its sources, for example through streams or batch loads.
3. **Processing**: data is cleaned, validated and transformed so it is usable.
4. **Storage**: data is kept in a suitable system, such as a database, warehouse or lake.
5. **Analysis**: data is examined for patterns, answers and predictions.
6. **Activation**: results are used to take action, such as personalizing an offer or triggering a process.
These stage names are generally accepted ones; the guide lists them without a Google page defining each.
## Applying it
The tap creates a record that did not exist a moment ago, which is genesis. Gathering it into central systems comes next and is collection.
W: Data genesis: the card tap on the reader is the event that first generates the travel record.
X: B=Processing: cleaning and transforming happens after the record has been created and gathered.
C=Analysis: examining patterns requires data that already exists and is stored.
D=Activation: this is the final step, where insights are used to act, not where data first appears.
T: Genesis is the first stage of the data supply chain: the moment data is created.

At which stage of the data supply chain is the gap, and what does it call for?

C: Data activation, which needs the dashboards redesigned so that managers see fewer production counts

- The sensors are producing real events, so genesis works.

W: Processing: duplicates, mixed units and missing values are exactly what cleaning, de-duplication and standardization are for, and they must happen before storage and analysis.

C=Activation: redesigning dashboards hides the symptom, but the numbers are wrong because the underlying data was never cleaned.

@ GCP-CDL D2 T2.1 applied troubleshooting
S: After acquiring two companies, a retailer finds sales data in one cloud, customer data in an on-premises system and product data in a third vendor's service. Reports need weeks of manual exports because the systems cannot share data easily. Which design principle best addresses this problem going forward?
A: Interoperability through open standards and connectors, so data can move and be queried across systems without manual copying
B: Isolation of each system, so that every department keeps strict control over its own data and rarely shares it
C: Standardizing on one closed ecosystem, so that every future acquisition must first rebuild its data in the vendor's format
D: Printing the weekly reports from each system, so that employees can combine the results by hand
K: A
G: ## Data silos and interoperability
A **data silo** is data trapped in an isolated system that other parts of the business cannot easily reach. Google Cloud lists silos as a governance risk because they stop the creation of a unified view and hold back analytics and AI.
## The remedy
**Interoperability** means that systems can exchange and use data with each other, usually through open standards, open formats and connectors. Google Cloud positions itself as open, which includes working with data across other clouds and on-premises environments, rather than requiring everything to move into one proprietary system. A manual export is the symptom of missing interoperability.
## Business payoff
- Faster, cheaper reporting.
- One view across acquired businesses.
- Freedom to choose or change systems later.
W: Interoperability with open standards: connected systems share data without manual exports, giving one view across acquisitions while keeping the freedom to change systems.
X: B=Isolation: keeping systems separate is how silos form, and it would make the reporting delays worse.
C=One closed ecosystem: forcing each acquisition to rebuild in a vendor's format is slow and recreates lock-in.
D=Printing reports: manual combination is the problem, not the solution, and it is slow and error-prone.
T: Interoperability based on open standards breaks down data silos and avoids lock-in.

G: ## Data for trends and strategic decisions
Two of the values the exam guide lists are identifying trends and informing strategic decision making. Combining data from many periods and sources shows patterns that no single person sees, such as a topic growing slowly for months, and lets leaders back major choices with evidence instead of instinct alone.
## Instinct and data together
- Experience suggests hypotheses; data tests them at scale.
- Trends over time need history kept in one analyzable place, which is what a data warehouse or lake provides.
- Evidence makes decisions explainable to boards and investors.
W: Evidence of trends: analyzing readership across time and topics reveals shifts in interest early, which informs what to commission and where to invest.
X: B=Legal record keeping only: reader data is routinely used to guide content decisions, so limiting it to record keeping wastes its value.
C=Instinct always wins: experience is useful, but it cannot detect slow, large-scale patterns, and the platform's purpose is analysis, not storing opinions.
D=Blocking viewers: restricting access is a governance concern, not the reason a central platform creates business value.
