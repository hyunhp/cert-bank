@ GCP-CDL D1 T1.1 applied definition
S: A newly hired board adviser asks the CIO of a manufacturer for a one-sentence definition of "cloud" that a non-technical director would accept. Which definition is the most accurate?
A: Computing resources such as storage and servers delivered on demand as services over the internet, with no self-managed hardware and pay-for-use billing
B: A private network of servers that a company buys, installs and operates in its own data centers, with capacity fixed at the level of the original purchase
C: A software license that lets a company copy its internal applications onto an unlimited number of employee laptops for offline use by its staff
D: A long-term hosting contract in which a provider reserves specific physical servers for one customer and bills a fixed fee regardless of use
K: A
G: ## What "cloud" means
Cloud computing is the on-demand availability of computing resources, such as storage and infrastructure, as services over the internet. The customer does not buy and look after the physical servers, the room, the power and the cooling. A provider owns and operates them, and the customer rents what it needs and pays for what it uses.
## The three ideas to remember
- **On demand**: capacity is requested when needed, not ordered months ahead through procurement.
- **As a service**: infrastructure, a development platform or a finished application is consumed, not owned.
- **Pay for use**: spending follows consumption instead of a fixed purchase.
## Reading the scenario
The adviser needs a plain definition. The right one names on-demand services, no self-managed hardware and pay-for-use. A company-owned data center is the opposite (on-premises).
W: On-demand services over the internet: it captures the three defining ideas, namely on-demand access, no self-managed physical resources, and paying only for what is used.
X: B=Own data centers: servers a company buys and runs itself, with capacity fixed by the purchase, describe on-premises infrastructure, which is what cloud replaces.
C=Software license for laptops: cloud is not a licensing scheme for copying applications to devices; it is about consuming shared computing resources as services.
D=Reserved servers at a fixed fee: dedicated hardware billed regardless of use is traditional hosting; cloud is defined by on-demand access and paying for use.
T: Cloud = computing resources as on-demand services over the internet, without self-managing the hardware, paid by use.
V: Google Cloud

@ GCP-CDL D1 T1.1 applied comparison
S: A marketing team used a generative AI tool to draft a promotional email. The head of digital now wants a system that, given the goal "raise sign-ups for the spring campaign", plans the steps, sends the emails through the company's marketing tools, watches the results and adjusts the plan on its own with little human intervention. Which term describes this system, and how does it differ from the earlier tool?
A: Agentic AI, because agents use a language model to plan and act in other systems toward a goal, where generative AI alone creates content from a prompt
B: Business intelligence, because it summarizes past campaign results in dashboards for people to read, where generative AI alone drafts the text of each email
C: A data warehouse, because it stores every campaign record in one place for analysts to query, where generative AI alone has no access to any stored information
D: Agentic AI, because it removes people from all decisions permanently, where generative AI alone can only be used inside a single spreadsheet or document
K: A
G: ## Generative AI versus agentic AI
**Generative AI** creates new content, such as text, images, code or music, from a prompt. The value comes from what the model can produce.
**Agentic AI** is centered on agents that use a large language model as the "brain" to perform actions through tools. It sets goals, breaks them into steps, acts in underlying systems and learns from the results. Google describes it as a subset of generative AI that goes beyond content creation and simple function calling.
## Example from Google
Generative AI can create marketing materials. Agentic AI can then deploy them, track performance and adjust the strategy based on the results.
## Agentic AI and AI agents
An AI agent is a building block that handles a specific task. Agentic AI is the coordinated use of several agents to run a wider workflow.
W: Agentic AI: the described system plans, acts in the marketing tools, measures results and adjusts, which is the goal-driven loop of agentic AI, while a content-only tool stops after producing the draft.
X: B=Business intelligence: BI reports on past data for people to act on; it does not plan steps or execute actions in other systems.
C=Data warehouse: a warehouse stores and organizes data for analysis; it neither plans nor acts, and gen AI is not defined by lacking access to stored data.
D=Removes people permanently: the term is right, but the reason is wrong. Agentic AI acts with minimal human intervention, not none; Google recommends human oversight, and gen AI is not limited to one spreadsheet.
T: Generative AI creates content; agentic AI uses agents to plan and take actions toward a goal.
V: Agentic AI

S: A retailer's CFO hears the CIO say that the company will "modernize its infrastructure" as the first step of its cloud program. The CFO asks what "infrastructure" refers to in this plan. Which description is correct?
A: The foundational resources applications run on, such as servers (compute), storage and networking, in a company data center or from a cloud provider
B: Only the customer-facing mobile app and website, because infrastructure means whatever shoppers see and touch when they browse and place an order
C: The set of company policies that decide which employees may approve purchases and sign contracts, independent of any technology the company uses
D: The reports and dashboards that managers read each morning, since infrastructure means the output of analytics and the charts built from sales data
K: A
G: ## Infrastructure in plain words
IT infrastructure is the foundation that applications and data depend on: compute (servers and virtual machines), storage, and networking, plus the facilities that house them. On premises, the company buys and runs it. In the cloud, a provider supplies it as a service.
## Why it matters for transformation
Modernizing infrastructure means using it more effectively, for example with containers or serverless computing, or by taking advantage of a cloud platform's global network, so that applications can be built and changed faster.
## Reading the scenario
The CFO is asking about the technical foundation, not about what customers see, internal policy or reporting.
W: Compute, storage and networking: these resources are the foundation applications run on, and "modernizing" them means moving to cloud services or more efficient designs.
X: B=Customer-facing app and website: these are applications that run on infrastructure; they are not the infrastructure itself.
C=Approval policies: governance and process matter in transformation, but they are not technology resources.
D=Reports and dashboards: these are analytics outputs that sit on top of data and infrastructure, not the infrastructure itself.
T: Infrastructure = the compute, storage and networking that applications run on, on premises or from a cloud provider.

S: A regional bank has moved its data center servers to a cloud provider with no change to its products, processes or customer channels. A new executive says the bank has "completed its digital transformation". Which response best reflects how Google Cloud defines digital transformation?
A: Not yet, because transformation changes business processes, culture and customer experience, and moving servers alone does not redesign how the bank works
B: Yes, because digital transformation means any migration of hardware to the cloud, whatever happens to products, processes and customer channels afterward
C: Yes, because digital transformation is complete once the number of physical servers owned by the company reaches zero, whatever else may have changed

Google Cloud defines digital transformation as using modern digital technologies, including public, private and hybrid cloud, to create or modify business processes, culture and customer experiences to meet changing business and market dynamics. @ GCP-CDL D1 T1.1 applied definition
S: A health-technology company wants to build its data platform on Kubernetes, a project whose source code is published for anyone to inspect, modify and share under a license that allows this. A board member asks what "open source" means for the company in business terms. Which answer is correct?
A: The source code is public, so anyone can inspect, modify and distribute it, a community can contribute and the company avoids dependence on one vendor
B: The software is always free of charge to run in production, and the company cannot pay anyone for support, hosting or managed services around it
C: The software has been certified by an international standards body, and every vendor must implement it in exactly the same way in every product
D: The software runs only on hardware owned by the company, because its code cannot legally be placed on a cloud provider's servers or services
K: A
G: ## What open source means
Open-source software is software whose source code is made publicly available under a license that lets people view, change and share it. Projects are usually developed with a community of contributors. Kubernetes, the container orchestration system behind Google Kubernetes Engine (GKE), is a well-known example that began at Google.
## Why a business cares
- **Portability**: an application built on open-source technology can run in several environments, which reduces dependence on one vendor.
- **Community innovation**: improvements come from many contributors.
- **Transparency**: the code can be inspected.
Google describes multicloud solutions built on open-source technologies like Kubernetes as providing the flexibility and portability to migrate, build and optimize applications across clouds and computing environments.
## Do not confuse
Open source concerns the availability of code. A formal agreed specification published for everyone to implement is an open standard.
W: Publicly available, modifiable source code: this is the definition, and the business benefit is community contribution and less dependence on one vendor.
X: B=Always free and unsupported: open-source code can be used without a license fee, but vendors sell managed services and support for it, so paying someone is allowed.
C=Standards-body certification: that describes an open standard, which is a published specification, not the availability of code.
D=Only on company hardware: open-source software can run on any suitable infrastructure, including cloud providers, and Kubernetes is run on clouds at scale.
T: Open source = publicly available code anyone can inspect, modify and share; its business value is portability and community innovation.
V: Kubernetes, GKE

@ GCP-CDL D1 T1.1 applied definition
S: A logistics company's systems must exchange shipment data with partners that use different vendors' software. The architect proposes that every system follow a publicly documented specification that any vendor can implement. What is this called, and what business benefit does it bring?
A: An open standard, which lets different vendors' products work together and makes it easier to switch suppliers without rebuilding every integration
B: A proprietary format, which locks every partner into one supplier so that the logistics company controls all exchanges between the parties involved
C: An open standard, which guarantees that all partners will use the same cloud provider, the same pricing model and the same support contract
D: A private cloud, which keeps shipment data inside one data center so that partners and outside systems cannot reach it or exchange it
K: A
G: ## Open standards
An open standard is a publicly documented specification, often maintained by a community or standards body, that anyone can implement. Because the rules of the interface are known, products from different vendors can work together (interoperability), and a customer can change suppliers without rebuilding every connection.
## Open standard versus open source
- **Open standard**: the agreed specification (how systems talk to each other).
- **Open source**: the publicly available code (how a product is built).
The two often go together, and both reduce lock-in because the interface and the code are not controlled by one supplier.
## Reading the scenario
The need is for different vendors to exchange data using a shared published specification, so the term is open standard, and its benefit is interoperability and freedom to switch.
W: Open standard with interoperability: a publicly documented specification lets different vendors' systems interoperate and lowers the cost of switching suppliers.
X: B=Proprietary format: a format controlled by one supplier is the opposite of an open standard and creates the lock-in the architect is trying to avoid.
C=Same cloud and pricing: the name is correct, but a standard does not force a single cloud provider or commercial terms; it makes systems work across providers.
D=Private cloud: a private cloud is a deployment model for hosting, not a specification for exchanging data.
T: Open standard = a public specification anyone can implement; it enables interoperability and less vendor lock-in.
V: Google Cloud

S: A toy retailer sells most of its products in the eight weeks before the holidays. Every year it buys enough servers for that peak, and the servers sit mostly idle for the rest of the year. The retailer is considering the cloud. Which benefit most directly addresses this problem?
A: Scalability, because capacity can rise for the seasonal peak and fall afterwards, so the retailer stops buying hardware sized for the busiest weeks
B: Data residency, because the retailer chooses the country where each customer's order records are stored during the busy season

Scalability means resources can be scaled up when demand rises and scaled down when it falls. On premises, companies buy for the peak, so utilization is low most of the year. ## Typical signs a company is a good candidate
- Demand varies widely, as in retail
- Low utilization of existing infrastructure
- Business growth that outpaces what the data center can supply
## Reading the scenario

C=Open standards: they help different products interoperate, but they do not change the need to buy peak-sized capacity in advance.

D=Call recording for audits: storage is a passive function; the agent does not respond or act.
T: Agentic AI perceives, reasons, plans, acts and reflects to pursue a goal with minimal human intervention.
