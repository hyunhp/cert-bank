# PLTR-DE batch 31: Domain 6 round 4 (Security, Projects, and Governance), 8 questions

@ PLTR-DE D6 T6.1 applied use-case
S: A dataset contains both personal data (names, emails) and non-sensitive operational columns. Two audiences need it: HR (everything) and operations (non-sensitive columns only). Which design uses the controls appropriately?
A: A single dataset with a PII marking, with operations staff granted the marking so that they can read the columns they need
B: A PII-marked full dataset for HR and an unmarked derived dataset without the personal columns for operations, with lineage
C: A single unmarked dataset with a Workshop view for operations that hides the personal columns on screen
D: Two copies of the full dataset in two projects, with operations asked not to use the personal columns in theirs
K: B
G: ## Derive, do not over-grant
The exam guide asks for least privilege per consumer and for markings on sensitive data. The right shape is two datasets with different sensitivity, not one dataset with everyone granted access.
## Design
- Full dataset: PII marking; HR and the pipeline identity hold it.
- Derived dataset: personal columns dropped; no marking; operations as Viewers.
- Lineage shows the relationship; a review confirms the derived dataset is non-sensitive.
Example: operations read 14 columns of operational data; names and emails never appear in their project.
W: Marked full dataset plus an unmarked derived dataset: each audience gets exactly its entitlement with mandatory controls intact.
X: A=Grant operations the marking: over-provisions dozens of people with personal data they do not need.
C=Workshop column hiding: a UI view is not access control; the columns remain readable through any other tool.
D=Two full copies: duplicates personal data and relies on a promise.
T: Separate sensitive and non-sensitive audiences with derived datasets, not by widening marking membership.
V: markings, projects

@ PLTR-DE D6 T6.2 applied troubleshooting
S: A pipeline references a dataset in another team's project. When the other team restructured their project into a new layout, the pipeline's input reference broke. How should cross-project dependencies be managed?
A: Copy the other team's dataset into your own project with a scheduled copy job so that their internal changes can never affect your pipeline
B: Reference the other team's published curated outputs (not internal datasets) under an agreed contract, with moves coordinated via lineage
C: Ask the other team to commit in writing never to change their project structure or dataset locations again
D: Rebuild the other team's transformation logic inside your own pipeline from their raw sources so that you no longer depend on them
K: B
G: ## Interfaces between projects
The exam guide asks for project boundaries and contracts between producers and consumers. Consumers should depend on published outputs, and producers should treat those outputs as stable interfaces.
## Practice
- Consume from the curated project, not from internal or raw datasets.
- Contract: schema, freshness, location; changes coordinated through lineage-based consumer discovery.
- Producers check downstream lineage before moving or renaming published datasets.
Example: the other team's internal reorganization no longer affects consumers because the curated output stays in place.
W: Depend on stable published outputs with a contract and coordinated change: cross-project dependencies survive internal reorganizations.
X: A=Copy their dataset: a stale duplicate that diverges from the source of truth.
C=Freeze their project: unreasonable; producers must be able to reorganize internals.
D=Rebuild their logic: duplicated effort and inconsistent results.
T: Consume other teams' curated outputs as stable interfaces; producers coordinate changes to them using lineage.
V: projects, Data Lineage

@ PLTR-DE D6 T6.3 applied use-case
S: A restricted view must show each sales rep their own accounts, their manager all accounts of their team, and the sales operations group everything. How should the policy be structured?
A: Three separate restricted views, one per audience, each backed by its own filtered copy of the data maintained by the pipeline
B: One restricted view whose policy combines: row's rep equals the user, or row's team is one the user manages, or the user is in sales operations
C: A marking per sales team applied to the whole dataset, with each manager holding their own team's marking and sales operations holding all of them
D: A Workshop module that filters the account list by the logged-in user's name, with a toggle that managers can use to widen the view
K: B
G: ## Composite policies
The exam guide covers restricted views for row-level access and their policy capabilities. Hierarchical access is expressed as a disjunction of attribute matches and group membership in one policy.
## Policy
- `row.rep_id == user.id`
- OR `row.team_id IN user.managed_teams` (from a user attribute or a mapping dataset).
- OR `user IN group:sales_ops`.
Example: a rep sees 40 accounts; her manager sees 300; sales operations see 12,000, all from one dataset and one view.
W: A single composite policy: all three audiences are served from one dataset without copies or dataset-level workarounds.
X: A=Three views on copies: triple the maintenance and the copies drift.
C=Markings per team: dataset-level; a manager would see nothing or everything, not their team's rows.
D=Workshop filter: a module filter can be changed by the user, and the data is readable through other tools; it is not access control.
T: Express hierarchical row access as one restricted view policy combining attribute matches and group membership.
V: restricted views

@ PLTR-DE D6 T6.4 applied troubleshooting
S: A dataset's owner reports that a contractor who left last month still appears to have access according to a report. What should the data engineer do?
A: Nothing, since the contractor's account was disabled when they left and the remaining entitlements are therefore irrelevant
B: Verify with Check Access, remove the stale roles, group and marking memberships through process, and fix the offboarding gap
C: Delete the dataset and recreate it from scratch so that no historical grants to anyone can possibly remain on it
D: Rename and move the dataset to a different folder so that the contractor could not find it even if the account were re-enabled
K: B
G: ## Stale access
The exam guide asks for least privilege and for diagnosing access with Check Access. Access that outlives its purpose is a control failure even if the account is disabled; group and marking memberships should be cleaned up.
## Response
- Check Access: confirm the entitlements.
- Remove: roles, groups, marking memberships via governed changes.
- Process: offboarding checklist that revokes platform access and memberships; periodic access reviews.
Example: the contractor remained in a group that grants Viewer on three projects; the group membership is removed and the offboarding process updated.
W: Verify, revoke through process, and fix the offboarding gap: the immediate issue and its systemic cause are both addressed.
X: A=Cannot log in: disabled accounts can be re-enabled; stale entitlements are still risk, and reviews will flag them.
C=Delete the dataset: destroys business data to fix a permission.
D=Rename: a moved dataset is still accessible to anyone with the entitlement; obscurity does nothing about the stale grant.
T: Remove stale entitlements through governed processes and fix offboarding so they do not recur.
V: Check Access, projects

@ PLTR-DE D6 T6.1 applied comparison
S: A project owner is trying to give a colleague read access to a dataset protected by a marking. Which action does NOT by itself grant the colleague the ability to read it?
A: Adding the colleague to the marking's membership through the marking's own approval process so they hold it directly
B: Granting the colleague the Owner role on the project that contains the dataset, which is the highest project role available
C: Adding the colleague to an existing group that already holds the marking as part of its entitlements
D: Removing the marking from the dataset after a documented review concluding that the data is no longer sensitive
K: B
G: ## Roles do not satisfy markings
The exam guide asks you to understand how markings and roles combine. A marking is mandatory: a role, even Owner, does not grant it.
- Marking membership (direct or via group) satisfies the marking.
- Removing the marking (after review) means there is nothing to satisfy.
- A project role alone leaves the marking requirement unmet.
Example: an Owner without the marking opens the project but cannot read the marked dataset.
W: Granting Owner: it changes the discretionary layer only; the mandatory marking remains unsatisfied.
X: A=Marking membership: holding the marking directly satisfies the mandatory control, so with a role the colleague can read.
C=Group with the marking: membership of a group that holds the marking satisfies it just as direct membership does.
D=Marking removed: no marking requirement remains, so a role suffices.
T: Only marking membership (or removal of the marking) satisfies a marking; roles never do.
V: markings, projects

@ PLTR-DE D6 T6.2 applied use-case
S: A team wants external consultants to build pipelines in Foundry for three months without being able to see production customer data. How should their access be structured?
A: Give the consultants Editor rights on the production projects and ask them in their contract to avoid opening any customer datasets
B: A development project with de-identified datasets where consultants have Editor rights and no production access; employees promote
C: Full administrator access on the platform for the duration of the engagement so that the consultants are never blocked by permissions
D: No platform access at all, with the consultants writing transform code on their own laptops and employees uploading it into repositories
K: B
G: ## Least privilege for contractors
The exam guide asks for project boundaries and least privilege. Development against de-identified data in a separate project gives contractors what they need without production exposure.
## Structure
- Development project: Editor for consultants; synthetic or de-identified inputs with the same schema.
- Production: no access; employees review pull requests and run branch builds on real data.
- Time-bound access that expires with the engagement.
Example: the consultants deliver three pipelines against de-identified data; an employee validates them on production branches and merges.
W: Isolated development project with de-identified data and employee-gated promotion: productive consultants, protected production data.
X: A=Editor on production with a request: a promise, not a control; and Editor could modify production pipelines.
C=Administrator: the most extreme over-provisioning possible for people who must not see customer data.
D=No access: impractical and loses the platform's development tooling.
T: Give external builders an isolated project with de-identified data and gate production changes through employees.
V: projects, markings

@ PLTR-DE D6 T6.4 applied troubleshooting
S: A build fails on a marked input although the build identity was recently added to the marking's membership. What should be checked?
A: Whether the grant took effect, whether the build's actual identity was the one added, and whether another requirement applies, via Check Access
B: Whether the dataset's files are corrupted, since failures on marked datasets often indicate underlying file problems rather than access
C: Whether the schedule ran too early in the day, since marking memberships granted during business hours only become active at midnight
D: Whether the transform's code needs an explicit marking annotation declaring that it is allowed to read the marked input
K: A
G: ## Verifying a grant
The exam guide asks you to diagnose access precisely with Check Access. A grant that "was made" may not be the grant the build needs.
## Checks
- Did the membership change complete (approval, propagation)?
- Was the right principal added? Builds often run as a service identity or group, not the engineer.
- Are there additional requirements (a second marking, an organization boundary)?
- Check Access on the build identity and the input shows which requirement still fails.
Example: the engineer was added, but the build runs as a service identity; adding the identity's group resolves it.
W: Confirm the grant took effect, targets the right identity, and covers every requirement, with Check Access: the precise way to find what still blocks.
X: B=Corruption: an access-denied error names a permission layer; corrupted files produce read or parse errors, not permission errors.
C=Time of day: marking memberships take effect when granted; there is no midnight activation or time-of-day dependency.
D=Code annotation: markings are governed in the platform, not declared in transform code.
T: After granting a marking, verify with Check Access that the exact build identity now satisfies every requirement.
V: Check Access, markings

@ PLTR-DE D6 T6.3 advanced tradeoff
S: A team is deciding between a restricted view and separate per-region datasets to serve regional analysts. Which considerations favour the restricted view?
A: Restricted views are always faster than querying separate datasets, so query performance alone decides the question in their favour
B: One pipeline and dataset, consistent logic, attribute-driven policies that follow user changes, simple lineage; with the all-rows caveat
C: Restricted views allow regional analysts to edit the underlying rows they can see, which separate per-region datasets do not allow
D: Restricted views do not require any permissions management at all once created, unlike datasets that need roles per project
K: B
G: ## When restricted views win
The exam guide asks you to understand restricted views and their limitations. Their strength is one governed dataset serving many audiences; their limitation is per-reader filtering, which affects pipeline reads.
## Considerations
- Maintenance: one dataset and policy versus N datasets and pipelines.
- Correctness: identical logic across regions.
- Dynamics: a user moving regions is handled by attributes, not by re-granting datasets.
- Caveat: pipelines needing all rows read the base dataset with proper access.
Example: 40 regions served by one view; adding a region is a policy update, not a new pipeline.
W: Single dataset, consistent logic, attribute-driven policies, simple lineage, with the all-rows caveat: the real reasons to choose a view.
X: A=Always faster: performance is not the deciding factor and not guaranteed.
C=Editing: views govern reading; they are not an editing mechanism.
D=No permissions: policies and base-dataset access must still be managed.
T: Prefer restricted views for multi-audience row access to keep one dataset and one logic; read the base dataset for full-row pipelines.
V: restricted views, projects
