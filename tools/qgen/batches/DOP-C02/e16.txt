G: ## RDS Storage Auto Scaling
Allocated storage on RDS is normally a fixed number you pick, such as 200 GiB. When free space runs out, writes fail. **Storage Auto Scaling** lets RDS grow the volume for you:
- You set a **maximum storage threshold**, for example 1,000 GiB.
- RDS increases storage when free space is low (roughly under 10 percent of allocated), the low-storage condition has lasted at least 5 minutes, and at least 6 hours have passed since the last storage change.
- Each increase is the larger of 10 GiB, 10 percent of current storage, or the predicted growth, and it happens online without downtime.
## How it caps cost
Growth never goes past the maximum threshold, so the largest possible storage bill is known in advance. Storage can grow but not shrink automatically, so choosing a sensible ceiling is the cost control. A `FreeStorageSpace` alarm is still a good companion to warn you when the database approaches the ceiling.
## Monitoring tools do not change storage
Note (2026-11-17): RDS Performance Insights reached end of life on July 31, 2026, and AWS moved its users to **Amazon CloudWatch Database Insights**. Like its predecessor, Database Insights shows load, wait events and top SQL; it never resizes or moves storage.
W: Storage Auto Scaling with a maximum: RDS grows storage automatically when it runs low, so nobody resizes manually, and the maximum storage threshold puts a hard cap on how large (and costly) it can get.
X: A=Alarm plus ticket: detects the problem but leaves a person to resize, which is the manual work the team wants to remove, and a sudden jump may fill the disk before someone acts.
B=Performance Insights: it analyzed database load, wait events and top SQL (now in CloudWatch Database Insights after its July 31, 2026 end of life); it never moved or reallocated storage between tables.
C=Multi-AZ: provides a synchronous standby for failover; the standby has the same storage size as the primary, and failover never adds capacity.
T: Automatic storage growth with a cost ceiling is Storage Auto Scaling plus its maximum storage threshold, not alarms, monitoring tools or availability features.

G: ## Where logs should live for long retention
CloudWatch Logs storage is priced for logs you actively search, and log groups default to **Never expire**, so costs keep growing. Amazon S3 storage classes are far cheaper for data you rarely read:
- **S3 Glacier Deep Archive** is the lowest-cost class, with retrieval in hours, which suits compliance archives.
## A typical design
- Set a short retention in CloudWatch Logs, for example 30 or 90 days, for troubleshooting and Logs Insights queries.
- Copy logs to S3 with export tasks (`CreateExportTask`) or continuously with a subscription filter to Amazon Data Firehose.
- Add an S3 Lifecycle rule: transition objects to Deep Archive after a few days and expire them after 7 years (about 2,557 days). Deep Archive has a 180-day minimum storage charge, which is irrelevant for 7-year data.
## Log classes are about ingestion
CloudWatch Logs has two log classes: **Standard** and **Essentials** (formerly named Infrequent Access; the API accepts `ESSENTIALS` or `INFREQUENT_ACCESS`). They differ only in ingestion price; storage is billed the same, so neither is an archive tier.
W: Short CloudWatch retention, S3 export, Deep Archive with 7-year expiry: recent logs stay searchable, the compliance copy sits in the cheapest storage class, and lifecycle expiry deletes it automatically when the 7 years end.
X: A=Recreate log groups every 30 days: deleting a log group deletes its logs, so the 7-year requirement is broken, and subscriptions, alarms and metric filters on it are lost each time.
B=Intelligent-Tiering on the log group: S3 storage classes apply to S3 objects, not to CloudWatch Logs log groups. Seven-year retention inside CloudWatch Logs would stay at CloudWatch storage prices.
D=Never expire with a built-in archival tier: CloudWatch Logs has no automatic cheaper archive tier for old data. The Essentials log class (formerly Infrequent Access) lowers the ingestion price only, with the same storage price, so Never expire keeps growing the bill.
T: Keep only the searchable window in CloudWatch Logs; move long-term compliance copies to S3 with lifecycle rules to Deep Archive and expiry.
