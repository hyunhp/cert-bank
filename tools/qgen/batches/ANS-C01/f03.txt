@ ANS-C01 D2 T2.1 applied use-case
S: A private VIF was created without changing its inbound prefix allocation, so it uses the default. How many IPv4 prefixes can the customer advertise to AWS over this VIF before the BGP session goes down?
A: Unlimited prefixes
B: Up to 1 prefix only
C: Up to 10 prefixes
D: Up to 100 prefixes
K: D
G: ## Prefix limits on Direct Connect VIFs
When your router advertises routes to AWS over BGP, AWS enforces a maximum number of prefixes per BGP session.
- **Private and transit VIFs**: each VIF has an **inbound prefix allocation** per address family. If you set nothing, as in this scenario, the default allocation is **100 IPv4 and 100 IPv6 prefixes**.
- With **inbound prefix controls** you can raise a VIF's allocation up to **1,000 per address family**, taken from the dedicated connection's pool (5,000 for 1 or 10 Gbps, 30,000 for 100 Gbps) and the Direct Connect gateway's 10,000 total. Higher values need your AWS Solutions Architect or TAM.
- **Public VIFs** keep a separate limit of 1,000 inbound prefixes.
- If you advertise more than the allocation, the BGP session goes into the **Idle** state (reported as DOWN), so all traffic on that VIF stops until you reduce the count.
## Staying under the limit
Summarize routes before advertising. Example: instead of 300 separate `/24` networks inside `10.20.0.0/15`, advertise one aggregate such as `10.20.0.0/15` or a handful of `/16` and `/18` summaries. If you truly need more routes, raise the VIF's allocation first; also remember that routes propagated into a VPC route table are still capped by VPC route table quotas.
W: Up to 100 prefixes: the VIF keeps the default allocation, which is 100 prefixes per address family for a private VIF. Advertising a 101st IPv4 prefix takes the BGP session to Idle; only raising the allocation with prefix controls (up to 1,000) would allow more.
X: A=Unlimited: AWS always enforces a per-VIF allocation to protect its routers, at most 1,000 per address family even after raising it; exceeding it takes the session down, which is why route summarization matters.
B=Only 1 prefix: you can advertise many prefixes over one private VIF; a single prefix is not a restriction.
C=Up to 10 prefixes: the default allocation is ten times higher, so designs advertising a few dozen prefixes work fine.
T: A private VIF on the default allocation accepts 100 customer prefixes per address family (up to 1,000 with prefix controls); exceeding the allocation idles the BGP session, so summarize or raise the allocation first.
