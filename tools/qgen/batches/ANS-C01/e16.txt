# ANS-C01 explain-only (docs drift 2026-11-17): Direct Connect inbound prefix controls (default 100 per VIF per address family, up to 1,000); letters are the published ones

@ Q001693 explain
G: ## Route limits on a Direct Connect virtual interface
A virtual interface (VIF) carries one BGP session between your router and AWS. AWS protects its side by capping how many prefixes you may advertise on that session.
- Private and transit VIFs now use **inbound prefix controls**: each VIF has a prefix **allocation** per address family. The default is **100** IPv4 and 100 IPv6 prefixes; you can raise it up to **1,000** per address family per VIF, drawn from the dedicated connection's prefix pool (for example 5,000 on a 10 Gbps connection) and the Direct Connect gateway's total of 10,000.
- If you advertise more prefixes than the VIF's allocation, AWS does not silently drop the extra routes: the BGP session goes down into the Idle state and the VIF stays down until the count is back under the allocation.
- Public VIFs are not managed by prefix controls and keep a limit of 1,000 inbound prefixes.
## Example
A data center migration adds 40 new `/24` networks on top of 80 existing ones on a VIF with the default allocation. The router now sends 120 prefixes, the session is torn down, re-establishes, receives 120 again and flaps.
- Fix: summarize, for example advertise `10.20.0.0/16` instead of many `/24`s, filter what you export, or raise the VIF's prefix allocation (up to 1,000) before advertising more routes.
W: Prefix allocation exceeded: the timing (right after new routes were advertised) and the AWS-side teardown match the documented behavior when a VIF receives more prefixes than its allocation (100 by default). Summarizing, filtering or raising the allocation restores a stable session.
X: A=DX gateway deleted: a Direct Connect gateway cannot be deleted while virtual interfaces are still attached to it, and removing gateway associations would not make AWS tear down sessions triggered by newly advertised routes.
B=Customer ASN changed: an ASN mismatch stops the session from establishing at all. Here the session was working and broke only when more routes were sent, which points to route volume, not peer identity.
D=BFD incompatible: Direct Connect supports BFD, and AWS enables asynchronous BFD on its side of every VIF. BFD only speeds up failure detection; it does not close sessions because new prefixes appear.
T: A VIF that drops right after you advertise more routes has gone over its prefix allocation (100 by default, up to 1,000 with prefix controls); summarize or raise the allocation instead of suspecting ASN, BFD or gateway changes.

@ Q003853 explain
G: ## Prefix limits on Direct Connect VIFs
When your router advertises routes to AWS over BGP, AWS enforces a maximum number of prefixes per BGP session.
- **Private and transit VIFs**: each VIF has an **inbound prefix allocation** per address family. If you set nothing, the default allocation is **100 IPv4 and 100 IPv6 prefixes**.
- With **inbound prefix controls** you can raise a VIF's allocation up to **1,000 per address family**, taken from the dedicated connection's pool (5,000 for 1 or 10 Gbps, 30,000 for 100 Gbps) and the Direct Connect gateway's 10,000 total. Higher values need your AWS Solutions Architect or TAM.
- **Public VIFs** keep a separate limit of 1,000 inbound prefixes.
- If you advertise more than the allocation, the BGP session goes into the **Idle** state (reported as DOWN), so all traffic on that VIF stops until you reduce the count.
## Staying under the limit
Summarize routes before advertising. Example: instead of 300 separate `/24` networks inside `10.20.0.0/15`, advertise one aggregate such as `10.20.0.0/15` or a handful of `/16` and `/18` summaries. If you truly need more routes, raise the VIF's allocation first; also remember that routes propagated into a VPC route table are still capped by VPC route table quotas.
W: Up to 100 prefixes: this is the default allocation for a private VIF, per address family. Going over it takes the BGP session to Idle; you can raise the allocation with prefix controls (up to 1,000), but out of the box 100 is the limit.
X: A=Unlimited: AWS always enforces a per-VIF allocation to protect its routers, at most 1,000 per address family even after raising it; exceeding it takes the session down, which is why route summarization matters.
B=Only 1 prefix: you can advertise many prefixes over one private VIF; a single prefix is not a restriction.
C=Up to 10 prefixes: the default allocation is ten times higher, so designs advertising a few dozen prefixes work fine.
T: A private VIF accepts 100 customer prefixes per address family by default (up to 1,000 with prefix controls); exceeding the allocation idles the BGP session, so summarize or raise the allocation first.
