@ SC-900 D4 T4.1 applied use-case
S: A bank's vendor-risk team must review independent evidence of how Microsoft cloud services are controlled before it approves Microsoft 365. It wants the SOC 2 report written by an external auditor. Where should the team get it?
A: The Microsoft Service Trust Portal, which publishes audit reports and compliance documents for Microsoft cloud services.
B: Microsoft Purview Compliance Manager, which produces an audit report about Microsoft from the bank's own tenant settings.
C: The Microsoft Defender portal, which lists the security incidents that Microsoft has investigated for each cloud service.
D: Microsoft Secure Score, which rates how well Microsoft itself has implemented controls in the shared cloud datacenters.
K: A
G: ## What the Service Trust Portal is
The **Microsoft Service Trust Portal (STP)** is Microsoft's public site for publishing **audit reports and other compliance-related information** about Microsoft's cloud services. Users can:
- download audit reports produced by **external auditors** (for example ISO/IEC certificates and SOC 1, 2 and 3 reports),
- read Microsoft-authored whitepapers on how Microsoft cloud services protect data,
- find guidance on managing cloud data security and compliance for their own organization.
## Why customers need it
Under the shared responsibility model, a customer cannot audit Microsoft's datacenters itself. Instead it relies on independent audit reports, which its own auditors and regulators accept as evidence of the provider's controls.
## Example
A vendor-risk analyst signs in to the STP, opens the SOC tile under **Certifications, Regulations, and Standards**, and downloads the latest SOC 2 report for Microsoft 365 to attach to the approval file.
W: Service Trust Portal: Learn describes the STP as the site where users download audit reports produced by external auditors, such as SOC and ISO/IEC reports, for Microsoft's cloud services.
X: B=Compliance Manager: it assesses the customer's own compliance posture with assessments and improvement actions; it does not generate a third-party audit report about Microsoft.
C=Defender portal: it is where a customer's security team investigates its own alerts and incidents, not a library of Microsoft's audit reports.
D=Secure Score: it measures the security posture of the customer's own tenant, not Microsoft's datacenter controls, and is not an auditor's report.
T: Third-party audit reports about Microsoft's cloud (SOC, ISO/IEC, FedRAMP) are downloaded from the Service Trust Portal.

@ SC-900 D4 T4.1 applied definition
S: A company moving to Microsoft 365 asks whether Microsoft will analyze its stored email to sell advertising or run marketing research. Which statement reflects Microsoft's privacy commitment that customers control their data?
A: Microsoft may use the content for advertising if the customer has not opted out in the tenant's privacy settings.
B: Microsoft can share the content with advertiser-supported services as long as names and addresses are removed.
C: Microsoft mines customer content for marketing research only in aggregate and only for its own product teams.
D: Microsoft does not mine customer data for advertising or marketing research and uses it only for chosen services.
K: D
G: ## Microsoft's four privacy commitments
Learn describes Microsoft's approach to privacy as a commitment, backed by contracts, across four areas: **data control, data location, data security and data defense**.
## You control your data
- Your data belongs to you: you can access, modify or delete it at any time.
- Microsoft does not use your data without your agreement; with agreement it uses the data **only to provide the services you have chosen**.
- Microsoft does **not share data with advertiser-supported services** or **mine it for marketing research or advertising**.
- Subprocessors who need access may perform only the functions they were engaged for and are bound by the same contractual privacy commitments.
- Compliance with standards such as **ISO/IEC 27018**, the first international code of practice for cloud privacy, reinforces this control.
W: No advertising or marketing use: Learn states Microsoft does not mine customer data for marketing research or advertising and uses data only to provide the services the customer chose.
X: A=Opt-out advertising use: Microsoft does not use the data for advertising at all, so there is no opt-out setting that would be needed to stop it.
B=Sharing after removing names: Learn says Microsoft does not share customer data with advertiser-supported services, without an exception for de-identified content.
C=Aggregate marketing research: mining customer data for marketing research is excluded entirely, including for Microsoft's own teams.
T: Customer data belongs to the customer; Microsoft uses it only to deliver chosen services and never for advertising or marketing research.

@ SC-900 D4 T4.1 advanced use-case
S: A European retailer's legal team asks what happens if a foreign government demands the retailer's data stored in Microsoft's commercial cloud. Which response matches Microsoft's commitment to defend customer data?
A: Microsoft hands the data over at once and informs the retailer after the government has closed its investigation.
B: Microsoft redirects the request to the retailer, notifies it where legally permitted, and challenges invalid demands.
C: Microsoft gives the government read-only access to the storage so that the retailer's applications keep working.
D: Microsoft deletes the data before responding so that nothing remains that the government could legally obtain.
K: B
G: ## Microsoft defends your data
The **data defense** commitment covers government and law-enforcement requests:
- Microsoft's position is that government requests for customer data should be **directed to the customer**, not to Microsoft.
- Microsoft **does not give any government direct or unfettered access** to customer data.
- Microsoft does not disclose data except as the customer directs or where required by law, and it scrutinizes each demand to ensure it is legally valid.
- **Where legally permitted, Microsoft promptly notifies the customer** and provides a copy of the request.
- Microsoft **challenges** government requests where it can lawfully do so, including in the courts.
These promises are part of Microsoft's standard contracts for commercial and public sector customers.
W: Redirect, notify and challenge: Learn says Microsoft directs requesters to the customer, notifies the customer with a copy of the request where legally permitted, and challenges demands that are not lawful.
X: A=Hand over at once: Microsoft scrutinizes every demand and discloses data only as the customer directs or where required by law, and it notifies promptly where permitted rather than after the fact.
C=Read-only direct access: Learn states Microsoft does not give any government direct or unfettered access to customer data.
D=Delete the data: deleting customer data is not part of the commitment; the data belongs to the customer, who controls access and deletion.
T: Under data defense, Microsoft points government requests to the customer, notifies where allowed, and challenges unlawful demands.

@ SC-900 D4 T4.1 applied definition
S: Which TWO statements describe how Microsoft secures customer data, according to its privacy commitments? (Select TWO)
A: Customer data is encrypted only in transit, because data at rest stays inside physically secured datacenters.
B: Data at rest is protected with encryption capabilities up to AES-256, and data in transit uses TLS and IPsec.
C: Two or more independent encryption layers are used so that no single point of compromise exposes the data.
D: Encryption keys are always held by Microsoft, and customers cannot manage their own keys or secrets themselves.

G: ## Your data is secured at rest and in transit
Microsoft's **data security** commitment uses advanced encryption for data both **at rest** and **in transit**:
- **At rest**: the Microsoft Cloud offers a wide range of encryption capabilities **up to AES-256**, so customers can choose what fits their needs.
- **In transit**: Microsoft uses and enables industry-standard encrypted transport protocols such as **TLS** and **IPsec**.
- **Layers**: **two or more independent encryption layers** protect against any single point of compromise.
- **Keys**: Microsoft-managed keys are properly secured, and technologies such as **Azure Key Vault** give customers control over passwords, encryption keys and other secrets.
## How it fits with the other commitments
Data security sits next to data control (ownership), data location (residency) and data defense (government requests).
W: B=At rest and in transit: Learn lists encryption up to AES-256 for data at rest and TLS and IPsec for data in transit.
C=Independent layers: Learn says two or more independent encryption layers protect against any single point of compromise.
X: A=Only in transit: Learn states data is protected both at rest and in transit; physical security does not replace encryption at rest.
D=Microsoft-only keys: Azure Key Vault and similar technologies give customers control over their own keys and secrets.
E=Premium license only: the encryption commitment applies to Microsoft's cloud services generally, not as a paid compliance add-on.
T: Microsoft encrypts data at rest (up to AES-256) and in transit (TLS, IPsec) with multiple independent layers, and customers can control keys.

A **control** is a requirement of a regulation, standard or policy. Compliance Manager tracks three types based on **who implements** it:

Improvement actions are separately described as **technical or nontechnical**, **mandatory or discretionary**, and **preventative, detective or corrective**. Those labels describe the action's nature and drive scoring, not who owns the control.

C=Mandatory and discretionary: these describe whether users can bypass an action, not whether Microsoft or the customer implements it.

@ SC-900 D4 T4.2 advanced comparison

G: ## How actions are weighted
Each improvement action gets a score value based on two questions.
**Can users bypass it?**

- **Preventative**: addresses specific risks before they happen (encryption at rest, separation of duties).
- **Detective**: monitors systems to find irregular conditions or intrusions (access auditing, compliance audits).
- **Corrective**: limits and reverses the effects of an incident (privacy incident response).

| Type | Points |
|---|---|
| Preventative mandatory | 27 |
| Preventative discretionary | 9 |
| Detective mandatory | 3 |
| Corrective mandatory | 3 |
| Detective or corrective discretionary | 1 |
