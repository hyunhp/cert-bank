Ground Truth is SageMaker's data labeling service. You define a labeling job (built-in task types include image classification, **bounding boxes**, semantic segmentation, text classification and more), point it at data in S3 and choose a workforce.
## Workforces
- **Private**: your own employees or invited members, for sensitive data.
- **Vendor**: third-party labeling companies from AWS Marketplace.
- Availability note: Amazon Mechanical Turk reached end of support on 2026-09-29, so the public Mechanical Turk workforce option is gone; Ground Truth jobs now use a private or vendor workforce.
## Automated data labeling
For supported task types such as bounding boxes, Ground Truth can train a model on human-labeled items as the job runs (active learning). Items the model labels with high confidence are labeled automatically; uncertain ones go to humans. On large jobs, such as 500,000 images, this can cut labeling cost considerably.
W: Ground Truth with automated labeling: it supports bounding-box jobs, a private workforce for sensitive images and automated data labeling that labels easy, high-confidence images by machine, which lowers cost at this scale.
X: A=Rekognition Custom Labels: it trains a custom detection model from labeled images; its console allows basic manual labeling, but it has no managed private workforce or automated data labeling for 500,000 images.
B=Raw Mechanical Turk: the public crowd is unsuitable for sensitive images, and using it directly means building the bounding-box tooling, quality control and automation yourself. Mechanical Turk also reached end of support on 2026-09-29, so it is no longer available at all.
D=Data Wrangler: Data Wrangler prepares and transforms tabular data for ML; it has no human labeling tasks, workforces or bounding-box annotation.
T: Large labeling jobs with sensitive data call for Ground Truth with a private workforce plus automated labeling; Rekognition Custom Labels only uses labels.

G: ## Ground Truth workforce types
When you create a labeling job you choose who does the work:
- **Private workforce**: people you choose, such as employees or contractors you invite. Members sign in through an Amazon Cognito user pool or your own OIDC identity provider, and only they see the tasks.
- **Vendor workforce**: third-party companies listed in AWS Marketplace that specialize in labeling; they work under their own agreements with you.
- **Amazon Mechanical Turk** (former option): an on-demand public crowd of anonymous workers, never suited to confidential or regulated data. Mechanical Turk reached end of support on 2026-09-29, so this public workforce option is gone and Ground Truth now offers private and vendor workforces.
## Why private fits healthcare
Medical images may contain protected health information. With a private workforce, only vetted staff who have agreed to your privacy obligations see the images, while Ground Truth still manages task distribution, the labeling UI and output collection.
W: Private workforce of employees or invited members: only people the company vets and authenticates can see the tasks, while Ground Truth still provides the managed labeling workflow.
X: B=No workforce restrictions: Ground Truth explicitly lets you choose who labels (a private or vendor workforce), so restricting who labels is a core feature.
C=Marketplace vendor with no privacy controls: vendor workforces are one option, not the only one, and they operate under agreements that can include confidentiality terms; this choice misstates both points.
D=Mechanical Turk: it was a public crowdsourced workforce, which the company has explicitly ruled out for privacy reasons; it has also been unavailable since Mechanical Turk reached end of support on 2026-09-29.
T: Sensitive data that still needs managed labeling goes to a Ground Truth private workforce; Mechanical Turk was the public crowd and has now closed.

@ Q003573 explain
G: ## Amazon SageMaker Ground Truth
Ground Truth is the SageMaker service for building labeled datasets. You point it at data in Amazon S3, choose a task type (image classification, bounding boxes, semantic segmentation, text classification and others) and a workforce.
- **Workforces**: your own private team or a vendor workforce from AWS Marketplace. (The Amazon Mechanical Turk public workforce option is gone: Mechanical Turk reached end of support on 2026-09-29.)
- **Annotation consolidation** combines answers from several workers per item to improve accuracy.
## Automated data labeling
For supported built-in task types, Ground Truth can use **active learning**:
- Humans label an initial subset.
- Ground Truth trains a model on those labels and uses it to label the items it is confident about.
- Low-confidence items go back to humans, whose answers improve the next round.
Example: for 50,000 product photos, humans might label only part of the set while the model labels the easy, confident ones, which lowers total labeling cost compared with sending every image to people.
W: SageMaker Ground Truth with automated data labeling: it combines managed human labeling workflows with active-learning-based machine labeling, which is exactly "humans plus automation to reduce cost".
X: A=Rekognition Custom Labels without labels: Custom Labels trains a custom image model from images you have already labeled; it consumes labels rather than producing them, so it cannot start from an unlabeled set.
B=Comprehend custom classification: Comprehend is a natural language service that classifies documents by their text; it neither labels images nor manages human annotators.
D=Glue DataBrew recipes: DataBrew cleans and transforms tabular data with recipes; it has no human labeling workflow and does not annotate image content.
T: Need human labels at scale with machine help to cut cost? Use Ground Truth with automated labeling; other services consume labels or process different data types.

@ Q000888 explain
G: ## Active learning in Ground Truth
Automated data labeling in Ground Truth works in rounds:
- Humans label a random sample of the data.
- Ground Truth trains a model on those labels and validates it.
- The model labels the remaining items; items above a confidence threshold get machine labels.
- Low-confidence items go to human workers, and their new labels are used to retrain the model for the next round.
Humans spend effort only where the model is unsure, which reduces cost and time while quality stays close to human labeling. It is available for built-in tasks such as image classification, bounding boxes, semantic segmentation and text classification, and is meant for large datasets: AWS documents a minimum of 1,250 objects and recommends at least 5,000.
## Contrast with Ground Truth Plus
Ground Truth Plus was a turnkey service where an AWS-managed expert workforce ran the labeling project for you; it was a different offering rather than the in-job feature this question asks about. Availability note: Ground Truth Plus reached end of support on 2026-06-30, while Ground Truth itself, including automated data labeling, continues.
W: Automated data labeling (active learning): it trains a model during the job, auto-labels confident items and routes only low-confidence ones to people, which is exactly the described feature.
X: A=Clarify bias detection: Clarify measures bias and explains model predictions; it does not label data or route examples to human labelers.
C=Ground Truth Plus only: Ground Truth Plus handed the whole project to an AWS-managed workforce; it was not the built-in feature that auto-labels items and sends uncertain ones to your reviewers, and it reached end of support on 2026-06-30.
D=Manual review with no automation: labeling everything by hand is the costly baseline the team wants to improve on.
T: To cut human labeling effort inside a Ground Truth job, use automated data labeling; Clarify is for bias, not labeling.

G: ## Continuous fairness monitoring
In a clinical setting, a model might start predicting a positive outcome (such as "needs follow-up") at very different rates for different demographic groups as the patient population changes. That needs to be watched continuously, not audited once.
SageMaker Clarify integrates with Model Monitor for **bias drift monitoring**:
- A baseline computes bias metrics for the facet (for example `ethnicity` or `age_group`), such as DPPL, the difference in positive prediction rates between groups, and sets allowed ranges.
- A monitoring schedule (for example daily) recomputes the metrics on captured endpoint data merged with the ground truth labels you upload.
- Violations are reported to S3, and metrics are published to CloudWatch, where alarms notify the team.
Example: at deployment, positive prediction rates for two groups differ by 0.02; three months later the gap is 0.11, the bias metric crosses its threshold and an alarm fires.
W: Clarify bias drift monitoring with Model Monitor: it recomputes group bias metrics such as the difference in positive prediction rates on production data on a schedule and raises alerts when they exceed the baseline limits.
X: A=Monthly JumpStart model evaluation: JumpStart evaluations assess foundation models on benchmark datasets; a monthly run is neither continuous nor based on this model's production predictions.
B=Feature Store online queries: the online store serves feature values with low latency; it does not compute disparities in predictions or raise alerts.
D=Ground Truth Plus reviews: Ground Truth Plus was a managed labeling service that produced training labels; it never measured bias in deployed model predictions, and it reached end of support on 2026-06-30.
T: Growing disparity in production predictions between groups is caught continuously by Clarify bias drift monitoring through Model Monitor, with CloudWatch alarms, not by a labeling service.
