@ GCP-GAIL D1 T1.1 expert tradeoff
S: A retail bank is designing a customer assistant. About 95 percent of questions are simple, such as opening hours or card limits, and must be answered within a second at the lowest possible cost. The remaining 5 percent are disputes that need careful reasoning over several documents. Reviewing a sample of those disputes, the team notices that most include photos of paper receipts and scanned statements. The bank has no team to run model servers. Which model strategy fits all of these requirements BEST?
A: Route simple questions to a fast, lightweight managed model and disputes to a more capable multimodal model, both on Agent Platform
B: Send every question to the most capable reasoning model, because using one large model for all traffic always lowers total cost
C: Send every question, including disputes, to the fast lightweight model, and ask customers to type out their receipts as plain text instead
D: Download an open model and host it on the bank's own servers for all traffic, so that every request costs nothing beyond the hardware
K: A
G: ## Matching models to tasks
Model choice weighs **performance**, **cost**, **latency**, **modality** and **availability**. A single model rarely optimizes all of them:
- Lightweight models answer simple questions quickly and cheaply.
- More capable models reason better over long or complex inputs but cost more and respond more slowly.
- **Multimodal** models accept images (receipts, scans) as well as text.
Many teams therefore **route** requests: simple traffic to a fast model, complex cases to a stronger one.
## The three constraints
1. Simple questions: sub-second answers at the lowest cost.
2. Disputes: careful reasoning, and (the detail) they arrive with photos and scans, so the stronger model must be multimodal.
3. No team to run model servers, so managed models on Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform fit, not self-hosting.
W: Route by complexity on managed models: the lightweight model keeps the 95 percent fast and cheap, while the multimodal model handles disputes with receipt photos, and Google runs both.
X: B=One large model for all: it raises cost and latency on the 95 percent of simple questions that a lighter model handles well.
C=Lightweight model for all: it may struggle with multi-document reasoning, and asking customers to retype receipts hurts the experience instead of using a multimodal model.
D=Self-host an open model: the bank has no team to run model servers, and hardware, scaling and patching are real costs.
T: Route simple traffic to a light model and complex, multimodal cases to a stronger one, using managed models when there is no ops team.
V: Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform

@ GCP-GAIL D1 T1.1 expert comparison
S: An airline wants its assistant to answer baggage and fare questions in the brand's formal style, using specific phrases the legal team approved. It has 30 approved example answers. Fares and baggage rules change every week, and the airline has no ML engineers. The legal team adds that each answer about a rule must point customers to the current policy page it is based on. Which TWO approaches should the airline combine to meet every requirement with the least effort? (Select TWO)
A: Prompt engineering with the approved examples as few-shot samples and clear style instructions in the prompt
B: Grounding answers in the current policy pages through retrieval, so responses reflect this week's rules and cite the page
C: Full fine-tuning on this week's fares and baggage rules, repeated every week so that the model memorizes the latest values
D: Prompt tuning on thousands of labeled conversations, which the ML team would collect and run before launch
E: Training a new airline-specific foundation model from scratch, so the brand style and rules are built in from the start
K: A,B
G: ## Prompt engineering, tuning and grounding side by side
- **Prompt engineering**: instructions and examples in the prompt. No training, no ML skills, immediate. Good for style, tone and format, especially with a few examples.
- **Prompt tuning / fine-tuning**: train on labeled examples to change model behaviour. Needs data, time and some ML skill; it bakes in what it learned, so it goes stale when facts change.
- **Grounding / RAG**: retrieve current documents at answer time and base the answer on them, with links to the sources.
## The three constraints
1. Style with 30 examples and no ML engineers: few-shot prompting.
2. Rules change weekly: retrieval of current pages, not memorized values.
3. The detail: each answer must cite the current policy page, which grounding provides.
W: A=Few-shot prompt engineering: 30 approved examples and clear instructions set the formal style with no training and no ML engineers.
B=Grounding in policy pages: answers come from this week's rules, and the retrieved page can be cited, as legal requires.
X: C=Weekly full fine-tuning: it is costly, needs ML skills, is stale between runs and cannot point to a source page.
D=Prompt tuning on thousands: the airline has neither the labeled data nor the ML team, and tuned behaviour still would not cite current pages.
E=New foundation model: far beyond the airline's data and skills, and it would still not know next week's rules.
T: Use prompts and examples for style, and grounding for changing facts with citations; tune only when prompting is not enough.
V: Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform

@ GCP-GAIL D1 T1.2 expert troubleshooting
S: A machinery maker built a grounded assistant over its service library so technicians can look up repair steps. The library holds scanned manuals, which were converted to text before indexing, plus several regional copies of each procedure. Some older manuals lack wiring diagrams. In testing, technicians report that the assistant sometimes gives two different torque values for the same bolt in one answer, and both values appear in indexed documents from different regions. Which data characteristic is the MOST direct cause of this problem?
A: Consistency, because regional copies of the same procedure disagree, so retrieval can surface conflicting values
B: Completeness, because older manuals lack wiring diagrams, so the assistant has to guess the missing torque values
C: Format, because the manuals started as scanned images, so the model cannot read any number in the indexed text
D: Cost, because licensing the service library is expensive, so the assistant was only given part of each procedure
K: A
G: ## Data quality characteristics
The exam guide lists **completeness**, **consistency**, **relevance**, **availability**, **cost** and **format** as characteristics that decide whether data is fit for AI.
- **Consistency**: the same fact is recorded the same way everywhere. Conflicting copies lead a grounded assistant to retrieve and repeat contradictions.
- **Completeness**: required information is present.
- **Format**: data is in a usable form; scans need conversion to text first.
## Reading the evidence
- Scanned manuals were already converted to text, and the values were found in indexed documents, so format is not blocking.
- Missing wiring diagrams are a completeness gap, but the symptom is about torque values, which are present.
- The detail: **both** values exist in documents from different regions. That is a consistency problem. The fix is to reconcile or retire conflicting copies and mark one authoritative version.
W: Consistency: conflicting regional copies are in the index, so retrieval can return both values and the assistant repeats the conflict.
X: B=Completeness: missing wiring diagrams are a real gap, but the torque values are present in two versions, so nothing is being guessed.
C=Format: the scans were converted to text and both values were retrieved, which shows the text is readable.
D=Cost: nothing in the scenario shows a partial library; the two values come from complete regional copies.
T: Contradictory answers from a grounded assistant usually trace back to inconsistent source data.

S: A sports league has 10,000 hours of past match footage with commentary, stored in Cloud Storage and growing by about fifty matches every week. Its media team wants editors to ask questions such as "show moments when the home side equalized late" and get written summaries of each match. Separately, marketing wants short promotional clips of imagined future-stadium scenes created from text descriptions. The archive and the clips must both be handled by Google's own model families. Which TWO pairings of need and model are correct? (Select TWO)
A: Gemini to analyze and summarize the existing footage, since it understands video, audio and text together
B: Veo to create the imagined stadium clips, since it generates new video from text descriptions or images
C: Veo to summarize the existing match footage, since a video model is required for any task involving video
D: Imagen to create the promotional clips, since it turns a text description into a short moving video sequence
E: Gemma to create the stadium clips, since its open weights let the league generate video on its own hardware

G: ## Understanding video versus generating video
Two different jobs involve video, and they belong to different model families:
- **Understanding** existing video (finding moments, summarizing, answering questions about footage and commentary) is a multimodal reasoning task: **Gemini** takes video, audio and text as input and produces text.
- **Generating** new video from a text or image prompt is **Veo**'s job.
- **Imagen** generates still images, not clips.
- **Gemma** models are open models for text (and some multimodal input); they are not Google's video generator.
## The trap
"Video in the task" does not mean "use the video generator". Ask whether the model must read video or create it.
W: A=Gemini for the archive: answering questions about and summarizing footage with commentary is multimodal understanding, a core Gemini strength.
B=Veo for the clips: imagined scenes from text descriptions are new video, which is what Veo generates.
X: C=Veo for summaries: Veo creates video; it does not watch footage and write summaries.
D=Imagen for clips: Imagen produces still images, so it does not create moving promotional clips.
E=Gemma for clips: Gemma's open weights do not make it a video generator; Veo is Google's video generation model.
T: Read video with Gemini; create video with Veo; create stills with Imagen.
V: Gemini, Veo

The security team's design review adds that every call into internal systems must be executed by the insurer's own application backend, which applies its own checks, never directly by the model or platform. Which TWO tooling choices meet these requirements? (Select TWO)
A: A data store over the policy PDFs, so the agent retrieves current passages when answering coverage questions
B: Function calling for booking: the model returns a structured request and the insurer's backend runs the call
C: An extension that lets the platform call the scheduling API directly whenever the model decides to book a new slot
D: Fine-tuning the model on the policy PDFs each quarter, so the agent recalls coverage rules without any retrieval
E: The Text-to-Speech API as the booking tool, so the model reads appointment requests aloud to the scheduling system
K: A,B
G: ## Agent tool types
Agents reach the outside world through tools. The exam guide names:
- **Data stores**: knowledge sources the agent searches, such as indexed documents, so answers reflect current content.
- **Functions**: the model is given function definitions and returns a **structured function call**; your application decides whether and how to **execute** it, then passes the result back. Execution stays on the client side.
- **Extensions**: connect the agent to an API that is called on the agent or platform side.
- **Plugins**: packaged integrations for common services.
## The three constraints
1. Answer from 3,000 PDFs that change quarterly: a data store.
2. Book in the scheduling system: an action tool.
3. The detail: calls must run in the insurer's backend, so the model proposes and the backend executes, which is function calling.
W: A=Data store: retrieval over the indexed PDFs keeps answers current each quarter without retraining.
B=Function calling: the model only proposes a structured booking call; the insurer's backend runs it with its own checks, as security requires.
X: C=Extension: the platform would call the scheduling API directly, which breaks the rule that the insurer's backend executes every call.
D=Quarterly fine-tuning: costly, stale between runs and not a lookup, so it is the wrong tool for changing policy text.
E=Text-to-Speech: it produces audio from text; it cannot book anything in a scheduling system.
T: Knowledge comes from data stores; actions that your own backend must execute use function calling.

G: ## Role prompting and system instructions
**Role prompting** tells the model who it is acting as (a persona) and in what tone. Google's prompt design guidance lists **system instructions** as a separate component: directives that control the model's behaviour across a set of tasks, usually set in a dedicated parameter. The guidance also warns: if the model is to act in a specific role, define that role **in the system instructions**.
Putting the role in the first user message mixes it with ordinary conversation, and later turns can pull the model away from it.
## The three constraints
1. Three assistants with different roles and tones on one model.
2. Model and budget are fixed, so no tuning or model switch.
3. The detail: the role must apply on every turn without users repeating it.
W: System instructions: each role lives in the system instructions, so it shapes every turn of every conversation and users repeat nothing.
X: B=Zero-shot with no role: it removes the persona the teams need, so the tone problem gets worse.
C=Higher temperature: more randomness makes tone less stable, not more faithful to a role.
D=Users repeat the role: it would partly work but shifts the burden to users, which the requirement rules out.
T: A role that must hold for the whole conversation belongs in system instructions.

@ GCP-GAIL D3 T3.3 expert tradeoff
S: A consumer-goods company runs two features on the same Gemini model through Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, and wants to fix both without changing models. The first brainstorms names for new snack flavours, and marketers complain that every run returns almost the same safe ideas. The second extracts clause details from supplier contracts into a fixed summary, and legal wants identical results for identical contracts. Testing logs also show that the long contract summaries regularly stop mid-sentence before the last clause. Which configuration addresses all three observations BEST?
A: Raise temperature for brainstorming; lower it for extraction and raise the maximum output tokens for the contract summaries
B: Lower temperature for brainstorming; raise it for extraction so that the summaries vary and cover more of each contract
C: Keep both temperatures unchanged and lower the maximum output tokens for contracts, so summaries finish sooner and more neatly
D: Raise top-p and temperature for both features, and tighten the safety settings so that contract summaries are never cut off
K: A
G: ## Sampling parameters and output length
- **Temperature** controls randomness in token selection. Higher values give more varied, creative output; lower values give more focused, repeatable output.
- **Top-p** (nucleus sampling) limits choices to the most probable tokens whose probabilities add up to the top-p value; lower values narrow choices.
- **Maximum output tokens** caps the length of the response; if it is too low, output stops mid-sentence.
- **Safety settings** block harmful content; they do not control length.
## The three observations
1. Brainstorming is repetitive: raise temperature for more variety.
2. Extraction must be consistent: lower temperature.
3. The detail: summaries stop mid-sentence, so the output cap is too low: raise maximum output tokens.
W: Per-feature settings: higher temperature adds variety to names, lower temperature steadies extraction, and a higher output cap lets summaries finish.
X: B=Swapped temperatures: it makes brainstorming even more repetitive and extraction less consistent.
C=Lower output cap: a lower limit cuts summaries off even earlier.
D=Higher randomness everywhere plus safety settings: it hurts extraction consistency, and safety settings do not control output length.
T: Tune temperature per task, and fix truncated answers with the maximum output tokens setting.

S: An insurer piloted a gen AI claims assistant with 50 adjusters for one quarter. Average handling time per claim fell 18 percent, and the CFO must decide on a full rollout. She wants evidence that the assistant caused the improvement. The pilot report mentions that claim volumes were unusually low that quarter after a mild season, and that every adjuster in the company used the same updated claims system during the pilot. Which measurement approach gives the MOST credible evidence?

B: Compare this quarter's handling time for the pilot group with the same group's figure from a busy quarter last year

D: Count how many prompts the pilot adjusters sent each day and treat higher usage as proof that handling time fell
K: A
G: ## Measuring the impact of a gen AI initiative
To show impact, link the solution to a **business KPI** (here, handling time per claim) and isolate its effect from other changes. Good practice:
- record a **baseline** before the pilot,
- compare with a **similar group without the tool** over the **same period**, so seasonal and system-wide changes affect both groups equally,
- add cost and quality measures (rework, accuracy) alongside time.
Adoption and satisfaction matter, but they do not prove business impact on their own.
## The three constraints
1. The CFO needs causal evidence, not just a lower number.
2. Claim volumes were unusually low (seasonal effect).
3. The detail: everyone got the same new claims system, which could also cut handling time.
W: Comparison group over the same period: both groups share the low season and the new claims system, so the remaining difference is attributable to the assistant.
X: B=Last year's busy quarter: the season differs and the new system was not in place, so the comparison mixes several causes.
C=Satisfaction survey: liking a tool is useful feedback but does not measure handling time or savings.
D=Prompt counts: usage shows adoption, not that claims were handled faster.
T: Prove impact with a business KPI, a baseline and a comparison group exposed to the same conditions.

@ GCP-GAIL D4 T4.3 expert use-case
S: A pension provider is launching a gen AI chat assistant for retirees. Its regulator requires customers to be told clearly when they are dealing with automated systems, and the board wants trust to grow rather than shrink. The assistant must also stay efficient enough to handle most routine questions. A customer research summary notes that many retirees feel anxious about technology and want to reach a person when a question concerns their savings. Which design BEST reflects responsible AI practice?
A: Tell users at the start that they are chatting with AI, explain its limits, and offer an easy handoff to a person
B: Give the assistant a human name and photo and avoid mentioning AI, so that retirees feel more comfortable with it
C: Mention the use of AI only in the website's terms of service, so that the chat itself stays short and uninterrupted
D: Remove all human contact options and route every question to the assistant, so that routine answers become faster
K: A
G: ## Transparency and accountability in customer-facing AI
Responsible AI in business includes **transparency** (people know when they interact with AI and what it can and cannot do) and **accountability** (people remain answerable, with a route to a human when it matters). Transparency builds trust; hiding AI erodes it when users find out.
Good practice for a customer assistant:
- disclose clearly, at the start, that the user is talking to AI,
- explain limits (for example, it cannot give personal financial advice),
- provide an easy path to a person for sensitive questions.
## The three constraints
1. Regulator: clear disclosure of automation.
2. Board: build trust while staying efficient for routine questions.
3. The detail: anxious retirees want a person for questions about their savings, so a handoff is essential.
W: Disclose, explain limits, offer a handoff: it meets the disclosure rule, keeps routine questions automated and gives anxious customers a person when they need one.
X: B=Human name and photo, no disclosure: it deceives users and breaks the regulator's disclosure rule.
C=Disclosure only in the terms of service: it is not clear disclosure in the conversation, so it fails the regulator's intent.
D=No human contact: it ignores retirees' need to reach a person about their savings and damages trust.
T: Be open that users are talking to AI, state its limits and keep a human within reach.
