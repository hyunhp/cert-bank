@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.3 applied comparison
S: An airline's customers currently open a help-center search box and read long articles to work out how to change a flight. The airline wants a digital assistant that talks with customers in chat or voice, collects the booking details, calls the booking system to complete the change, and hands off to a human when needed. Which Google Cloud offering fits this goal best?
A: Conversational Agents, which build virtual agents that hold multi-turn chat or voice conversations and connect to backend systems
B: Agent Search, which returns ranked answers from the help-center articles but does not carry out transactions in the booking system
C: Grounding with Google Search, which lets a model answer from public web pages so customers need not read the help center
D: Conversational Insights, which analyzes past conversations to show managers why customers ask to change flights
K: A
G: ## Finding information versus completing a task
Customer-experience needs fall into a few families, and each Google Cloud offering is built for one of them:
- **Search** (Agent Search): a customer asks a question and gets relevant results or a generated answer drawn from content. The goal is to find or understand information.
- **Conversational Agents**, part of the Customer Engagement Suite: a virtual agent that holds a dialogue, collects the details it needs, calls other systems, and escalates to a person. The goal is to get something done. (Documentation also shows the earlier name Dialogflow CX and the newer Gemini Enterprise for Customer Experience branding for the suite.)
- **Analysis** (Conversational Insights): learning from conversations that already happened.
## Reading the scenario
The decisive phrases are "collects the booking details" and "calls the booking system to complete the change". Those describe a transaction inside a conversation, not a lookup.
W: Conversational Agents: it is designed for virtual agents that converse over chat or voice, gather the required information, use tools or backend systems to act, and pass the customer to a human, which is the whole workflow described.
X: B=Agent Search: it improves finding answers in the help center, which the airline already has in a basic form, but it is a search and answer experience, not a dialogue that completes a booking change.
C=Grounding with Google Search: it supplies a model with public web information; it neither knows the airline's booking rules nor acts on its booking system.
D=Conversational Insights: it analyzes conversations after the fact for managers; it does not talk with customers.
T: Customer needs a task completed through conversation → Conversational Agents; needs information found → search.
V: Conversational Agents, Agent Search

@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.3 applied use-case
S: A contact center has many new representatives who often put customers on hold while they look up policies and then spend several minutes writing a summary after each call. Customers are already served by a virtual agent for simple requests. Management wants the human representatives to resolve the remaining calls faster and more consistently. Which offering addresses this need?
A: Conversational Insights, which reports weekly on call topics and sentiment so supervisors can schedule extra training sessions
B: Agent Assist, which gives representatives real-time suggested answers from a knowledge base and automatic summaries of the call
C: A second Conversational Agents virtual agent, which handles the policy questions so representatives no longer receive them
D: Agent Search on the public website, which lets customers look up the policies themselves before they call
K: B
G: ## Helping the human in the loop
Not every contact can be automated. Some stay with human representatives, and the business value is then in making each representative faster and more consistent. **Agent Assist** is the Customer Engagement Suite offering for that: while a representative is on a call or chat, it can transcribe the conversation in real time, recommend ready-to-send responses and next steps, surface answers from a centralized knowledge base, and produce a summary at the end of the interaction. New staff learn from the same guidance, so quality is more even.
## Contrast with the other offerings
- A virtual agent replaces or defers a human contact; Agent Assist supports one who is already on the contact.
- Analytics tools explain trends across many contacts; they do not help during a single call.
W: Agent Assist: it works alongside the representative, offering suggested answers drawn from knowledge and automatic summaries, which directly cuts the hold time spent searching and the time spent writing wrap-up notes.
X: A=Conversational Insights: it shows patterns after interactions are over, which helps planning and coaching, but it gives nothing to a representative in the middle of a call.
C=Second Conversational Agents agent: the policy questions that reach humans are the ones the virtual agent did not resolve, and adding another agent does not shorten how long a representative spends on hold or on notes.
D=Agent Search on the website: it may deflect a few customers, but it leaves the hold time and the manual summaries of calls that do arrive unchanged.
T: Faster, more consistent human representatives during live contacts → Agent Assist.
V: Agent Assist

@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.3 applied use-case
S: A telecom operator records hundreds of thousands of customer calls and chats each month. Operations leaders cannot read them all, yet they want to know the main reasons customers contact the company, how sentiment differs by topic, and which interactions deserve a quality review. Which offering should they use?
A: Agent Assist, which suggests answers to representatives during each call so that fewer topics need later analysis
B: Conversational Agents, which will answer each contact and thereby remove the need to study why customers call
C: Conversational Insights, which applies analytics to conversation data to surface topics, sentiment and interactions worth reviewing
D: Grounding with Google Search, which compares the operator's call topics with trends seen on the public web
K: C
G: ## Learning from conversation data
Contact centers produce a large volume of unstructured data: call recordings and chat transcripts. **Conversational Insights** (called Customer Experience Insights in current documentation) lets a business import that data and run analytics on it. It can identify call topics, entities and caller and representative sentiment, flag interesting interactions for review, and let a reviewer play back audio synchronized with the transcript. The results can be exported for further analysis. It can import conversations from Conversational Agents and Agent Assist, so it closes the loop between serving customers and improving the service.
## Business value
Leaders see call drivers, so they can fix the product or process that causes avoidable calls, target training, and focus quality teams on the conversations that matter instead of random samples.
W: Conversational Insights: it analyzes conversation data at scale to reveal topics, sentiment and interactions that need review, which is exactly the visibility the leaders lack.
X: A=Agent Assist: it supports a representative during a live contact; it does not give leaders an aggregate view of why customers call.
B=Conversational Agents: automating answers may change who handles a contact, but without analysis the company still would not know which issues drive volume or how customers feel.
D=Grounding with Google Search: it connects a model to public web information; the operator's own call topics and sentiment are not in it.
T: Understand why customers contact you and where quality falls short → Conversational Insights.
V: Conversational Insights

Teams can set it up with the console or the Discovery Engine API and embed the result in a website through a widget. For retail-specific needs, documentation also lists an AI Commerce Search offering for search, browse and recommendations.

A shop must show its own products, prices and availability, so the search has to run over its catalog, not the open web.
W: Agent Search: it supplies natural-language search, similar-item recommendations and generative answers over the shop's own content, and offers a website widget that suits a small team.

@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.3 applied use-case
S: A travel-advice company is building an assistant whose answers must reflect current events such as a newly announced strike, a weather alert or a recently opened attraction. The company has no internal document set that covers these topics. Which capability should it use to keep answers current?
A: Fine-tuning the model once a year on the company's older travel brochures so that it learns recent facts
B: Conversational Insights, which turns past customer conversations into up-to-date destination information
C: Agent Search over an empty data store, which will fill itself with current events as customers ask questions
D: Grounding with Google Search, which connects the model to current public web information when it answers
K: D
G: ## When the answer lives on the public web
A model knows only what was in its training data, so it can be out of date. **Grounding** connects a model to an outside source at answer time. Google Cloud offers different sources for different needs:
- **Grounding with Google Search**: use it when you want to connect the model to world knowledge across a wide range of topics or to up-to-date information on the internet. The data is publicly available web data.
- **Agent Search and RAG over your own data**: use them when answers must come from the organization's private or proprietary content.
## Applying it
Strikes, weather and new openings are public, fast-changing facts and the company holds no private source for them, so Google Search is the natural grounding source.
W: Grounding with Google Search: it brings current public web information into the model's answer, which is what time-sensitive travel facts require.
X: A=Annual fine-tuning: a model tuned once a year is stale again within days, and brochures that predate the events cannot contain them.
B=Conversational Insights: it analyzes the company's own contact-center conversations for operations; it is not a source of current destination news.
C=Agent Search over an empty store: a data store only searches content that has been added to it; it does not collect current events by itself.
T: Current, public, world-knowledge facts → Grounding with Google Search; private company content → Agent Search or RAG.
V: Grounding with Google Search, Agent Search

@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.3 applied tradeoff
S: A software-as-a-service provider launches a public support assistant. Customers ask about the provider's own refund rules and contract terms. In testing, the assistant, which is grounded with Google Search, sometimes quotes a competitor's policy that it found on the web. What is the best fix?
A: Ground the assistant in the provider's own policy and help content with Agent Search so that answers come from approved sources
B: Raise the number of web results used for grounding, since more results will eventually include the provider's own rules
C: Add Conversational Insights so that supervisors can read the wrong answers after they have been given to customers
D: Replace the assistant with Agent Assist so that customers talk to a representative who sees suggested responses
K: A
G: ## Matching the grounding source to the question
Grounding with Google Search draws on publicly available web data. That is right for open-ended, current, world-knowledge questions and wrong for questions whose answer is a company's own, specific rule. A model grounded in the whole web can find a plausible but irrelevant or competing source.
**Agent Search** grounds and searches over content the organization chooses, such as its websites, help articles and documents, so the assistant answers from approved material and can be refreshed when policies change.
## Trade-off
Restricting to approved content gives accuracy and control for company-specific facts but gives up the breadth of the open web; most customer-support assistants should choose accuracy.
W: Agent Search with the provider's content: it limits answers to the provider's own approved policies and help articles, which removes the competitor's policy from the source pool.
X: B=More web results: widening the pool of public sources makes a mix-up with other companies' terms more likely, not less, because the provider's refund rules are only one page among many.
C=Conversational Insights: it analyzes conversations after the fact; it would reveal the mistakes but not prevent them, so customers would still receive wrong answers.
D=Agent Assist: it supports human representatives; switching to it abandons the self-service goal and does not by itself change the source of answers.
T: Company-specific answers need company-approved sources (Agent Search); the open web is for public, current facts.
V: Agent Search, Grounding with Google Search

@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.3 advanced troubleshooting
S: A utility company launched a virtual agent for billing questions. Executives are pleased that simple questions are answered, but customer satisfaction on escalated calls is still low, and nobody can explain why customers keep escalating. Which next step gives the best insight into the cause?
A: Analyze the stored conversations with Conversational Insights to find the topics and sentiment in the escalated contacts
B: Replace the virtual agent with Grounding with Google Search so that answers come from the public web
C: Add Agent Search to the public site, since a search box always reduces the number of customers who escalate
D: Stop recording conversations so that fewer data privacy questions arise, and rely on executives' impressions

G: ## Closing the loop with analytics
Automation can leave a business blind: it resolves the easy contacts, and the remaining contacts are harder and more emotional. To improve, a team needs to see what is actually happening in those conversations. **Conversational Insights** can import conversation data, including from Conversational Agents, and apply machine learning analytics to find call topics, entities and sentiment, and to flag interactions for review. Reviewers can play the audio alongside a synchronized transcript.
## What the data answers
Why do customers escalate: a missing intent, a confusing answer, an unresolved billing topic? Analysis of the real conversations replaces guesswork, and the findings guide changes to the virtual agent's design or to the representatives' training.
W: Conversational Insights: it reveals which topics and sentiments are associated with escalations, so the team can improve the virtual agent where customers actually struggle.
X: B=Grounding with Google Search: public web content cannot explain a particular utility's billing escalations and would risk answers unrelated to its customers' accounts.
C=Agent Search on the site: it might help customers find articles, but it provides no diagnosis, and "always" overstates what a search box achieves.
D=Stop recording: removing the data removes the only evidence of what happens; privacy is handled with controls and policy, not by giving up insight.
T: Unknown reasons behind escalations or low satisfaction → analyze real conversations with Conversational Insights.
