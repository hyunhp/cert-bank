@ GCP-GAIL D1 T1.1 advanced troubleshooting
S: A new team's first generative AI pilot produced poor results. A review found that the team skipped data preparation and fed raw, inconsistent records directly into training. What is the most accurate lesson?
A: Preparation can be skipped with larger models, since they detect and correct inconsistent or duplicate records in training
B: Preparation belongs after deployment, once the model is in production and its outputs can be monitored for quality issues
C: A model learns whatever patterns its inputs contain, so records have to be cleaned and standardized before training starts
D: Switching from supervised to reinforcement learning is the fix, because it learns from rewards rather than from the records
K: C
G: ## Garbage in, garbage out
A model learns the patterns in its data, including errors, duplicates, gaps and inconsistent formats. Data preparation (cleaning, standardizing, deduplicating, labeling where needed) is therefore a prerequisite step that influences all later stages. Model size does not remove the issue.
## Position in the lifecycle
Preparation sits between ingestion and training. Skipping it hurts training results, and problems then show up in deployment as unreliable output.
W: Data quality drives model quality: the model learns errors and inconsistencies along with everything else, so cleaning and standardizing before training is how the pilot's poor results are prevented.
X: A=Bigger models fix it: larger models can learn flawed patterns just as well, and they do not repair inconsistent records.
B=After deployment: monitoring is important but cannot replace preparing data before training.
D=Switch to RL: changing the learning approach does not address defective input data.
T: Prepare and clean data before training; poor data produces poor models.

@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.3 applied use-case
S: A retailer is replacing its aging call center platform. It wants one cloud-native platform that handles voice, chat, SMS and email, keeps the context of a customer's conversation when the customer switches channels, and has AI self-service and assistance for representatives built in. Which offering fits?
A: Agent Search, which lets customers search the retailer's site and app content and get generative AI answers
B: Contact Center as a Service, an omnichannel offering that combines AI agents, routing and an agent desktop
C: Conversational Insights, which analyzes customer conversations across channels to surface topics and sentiment
D: Grounding with Google Search, which connects a model's answers to current information from the public web
K: B
G: ## Individual building blocks and the platform that joins them
The Customer Engagement Suite has AI building blocks, each solving one problem: Conversational Agents for virtual agents, Agent Assist for live support to representatives and Conversational Insights for analytics. A business that needs the whole contact center, not just one capability, can adopt **Google Cloud Contact Center as a Service (CCaaS)**.
CCaaS is a cloud-native, omnichannel contact center solution. It covers voice, chat, SMS, email and in-app experiences, AI-driven routing, an agent desktop that can also work inside a CRM, and workforce and quality-management capabilities. It uses the suite's building blocks for automated responses and insights, and it aims to let customers move between channels without losing context.
## Reading the scenario
"Replacing the platform", "voice, chat, SMS and email" and "keeps context when switching channels" all point to a platform, not to a single AI feature.
W: Contact Center as a Service: it is the complete omnichannel contact center with AI built in, so it replaces the legacy platform and preserves context across channels.
X: A=Agent Search: it improves finding information on a site or app; it does not receive calls, route contacts or provide an agent desktop.
C=Conversational Insights: it analyzes interactions after they take place and does not route or answer incoming contacts, so it cannot replace the platform.
D=Grounding with Google Search: it is a way to supply a model with public web knowledge, not a platform for any customer channel.
T: Replace or modernize the whole contact center, omnichannel with AI → Contact Center as a Service.
V: Contact Center as a Service
