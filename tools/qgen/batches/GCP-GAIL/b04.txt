@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.1 applied use-case
S: A retailer's AI team wants to test a Google model, an open-source model and a partner model on the same product-description task before committing to one. The CIO wants this comparison done on a single managed platform rather than by stitching together three vendors' tooling. Which strength of Google Cloud's approach addresses this need?
A: An open approach in which Model Garden offers Google, open-source and partner models side by side to discover, test, customize and deploy
B: A closed approach in which every workload must use a single Google-built model, so that results are consistent across all teams
C: An infrastructure-only approach in which Google supplies the servers and the retailer hosts each model with its own tooling
D: A pre-built application approach in which a finished Google app is configured by users and no model comparison is possible
K: A
G: ## What "open approach" means on Google Cloud
Google Cloud describes its approach to gen AI as open: the platform does not force one model on you. **Model Garden** in Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform (formerly Vertex AI) is a library where teams discover, test, customize and deploy models and assets from Google and Google partners, including open-source models.
## Why it matters to a business
- **Choice**: pick the best model for each task instead of the one model a vendor owns.
- **Less lock-in**: a model that falls behind can be replaced.
- **One place to work**: evaluation and deployment use one managed platform, not separate tooling per vendor.
## Reading the scenario
The need is comparing several model sources on one platform. That is the open-approach benefit, not raw hardware or a finished app.
W: Open approach with Model Garden: it lists Google, open-source and partner models in one library, so the team can try each on the same task and deploy the winner on the same managed platform.
X: B=Single-model approach: this is the opposite of Google's stated open approach, and it would make the comparison the CIO asks for impossible.
C=Infrastructure only: Google Cloud does sell infrastructure, but the retailer would host and tool each model itself, which is the vendor stitching the CIO wants to avoid.
D=Finished app only: a pre-built application suits end users, but it is not where models from different providers are compared and deployed.
T: Open approach = a choice of Google, open-source and partner models on one managed platform.
V: Model Garden, Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform

@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.1 applied use-case
S: A bank has built agents on a platform that lets it change the underlying model whenever a better or cheaper one appears. The risk team asks what the bank gives up each time the model is changed. Which statement reflects the benefit Google describes for its open, multi-model platform?
A: The bank must rebuild its tool connections and identity policies each time, but gains a better model with every change
B: The bank can swap models while keeping its underlying logic, tool connections and identity policies intact
C: The bank can swap only between Google-built models, because partner and open-source models use incompatible controls
D: The bank can swap models only at the end of a contract term, when Google re-provisions the agents on the new model
K: B
G: ## Avoiding lock-in
A common worry with gen AI is that today's best model is not tomorrow's. If the model, the tools and the security rules are welded together, every model change becomes a project.
Google Cloud describes its AI platform as one where teams can access Google, third-party and open-source models and switch among them to capture new performance or lower cost. The agent logic, the tool connections and the identity policies sit around the model, so they stay in place when the model changes.
## Why this is a business strength
- Faster adoption of better models.
- Lower switching cost, which strengthens negotiating position.
- Governance stays consistent because policies are not tied to one model.
W: Swapping with logic, tools and identity policies intact: this is the stated benefit, so the bank can adopt a better or cheaper model without redoing the work around it.
X: A=Rebuild each time: this describes the lock-in the platform is meant to avoid; the point is that connections and policies are kept.
C=Google models only: Google positions the platform as giving access to Google, third-party and open-source models side by side, so switching is not limited to Google-built ones.
D=Only at contract end: nothing ties model changes to a contract term; the benefit is that the bank can change whenever it wants.
T: Open multi-model platform = change the model without rebuilding logic, tool connections or identity policies.
V: Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform

@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.1 applied use-case
S: A healthcare company wants to use Google's gen AI services with its own confidential documents. Its legal team asks first whether Google will use the company's data to train or fine-tune models without being asked. Which statement describes Google Cloud's enterprise-ready position?
A: Google may use the prompts for training by default and the company must file a request each month to opt out of it

C: Google does not use customer data to train or fine-tune AI/ML models without the customer's prior permission or instruction

Google Cloud presents its AI platform as responsible, secure, private, reliable and scalable. For regulated industries the first question is usually about data: where does it go, and who can learn from it?

D=Premium tier required: the training restriction is part of the service terms, not a benefit sold in a higher support tier.
T: Enterprise-ready includes privacy: no training or fine-tuning on customer data without prior permission or instruction.

@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.1 applied use-case
S: A financial regulator requires a bank to control the encryption keys that protect the data it stores for its gen AI projects, including their location, rotation schedule and who may use them. By default, Google encrypts the content at rest. Which capability meets the requirement?
A: CMEK in Cloud KMS, giving the bank control over key protection level, location, rotation and access
B: Google default encryption, which already gives the bank direct control of rotation schedules and key access permissions
C: A prompt-engineering policy that tells employees not to include sensitive details in the text sent to a model
D: A larger model choice, because bigger models apply stronger encryption to the data they process

G: ## Two levels of encryption control
- **Google default encryption**: Google encrypts customer content at rest automatically. Nobody has to configure it, and the customer does not manage the keys.
- **Customer-managed encryption keys (CMEK)**: the customer creates and controls keys in Cloud KMS. Using Cloud KMS keys gives control over their protection level, location, rotation schedule, usage and access permissions.
## Why this is part of an enterprise-ready platform
Regulated organizations often must show that they, not the provider, control key lifecycle. Platform support for CMEK lets them do that on AI resources without leaving the managed service.
W: CMEK in Cloud KMS: it moves key control to the bank, covering the protection level, location, rotation and permissions the regulator names.
X: B=Default encryption: it protects data at rest but Google manages the keys, so the bank does not get the control the regulator requires.
C=Prompt policy: a staff guideline reduces what is sent but does not control the keys that protect stored data.
D=Larger model: model size has no relation to how stored data is encrypted or who controls the keys.
T: Need to control keys → CMEK; default encryption is automatic but Google-managed.
V: Cloud KMS, Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform

@ GCP-GAIL D2 T2.1 applied use-case
S: A research lab will train a very large model whose computation consists mostly of large matrix operations. The lab asks what Google-designed hardware exists for this. Which hardware, and which benefit, should the cloud adviser describe?
A: Cloud TPUs, custom chips for large matrix operations with high-bandwidth memory, connectable in slices
B: General-purpose CPUs only, because matrix-heavy models are best trained on processors built for flexibility with many small tasks
C: Local hard disks on each server, because training speed depends mainly on where the training files are stored
D: Edge devices at customer sites, because distributing the training across users' phones is how Google scales large models
K: A
G: ## Tensor Processing Units
**TPUs** are Google's custom-developed, application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) built to accelerate machine learning. They suit models dominated by matrix computations, which is the core operation in neural networks.
- They have on-chip high-bandwidth memory (HBM), allowing larger models and batch sizes.
- They can be connected in groups called **slices**, which scale up workloads with little to no code changes.
## Business meaning
Custom silicon is part of what Google calls AI-optimized infrastructure. For a business it means faster training and serving of large models on hardware designed for that purpose.
W: Cloud TPUs: they are Google-designed for matrix-heavy workloads, with high-bandwidth memory for large models and slices for scale.
X: B=CPUs only: CPUs fit quick prototyping and small models, not large matrix-dominated training.
C=Local disks: storage matters, but disks do not perform the computation, and the question is about processing hardware.
D=Edge devices: Google's training infrastructure is data center accelerators, not phones at customer sites.
T: Matrix-heavy large-model work → TPUs: custom chips, high-bandwidth memory, slices for scale.
V: Cloud TPU

Auditing depends on knowing who did what. Google Cloud assigns each agent a cryptographically secure identity (SPIFFE-based) with least-privilege IAM controls. This provides traceability and auditing logs for model calls, prompts and queries across the agent's lifecycle.
- **Identity** answers "which agent acted?"
- **Least privilege** answers "what could it reach?"
## Why shared accounts fail
A shared account hides which agent acted, and unlimited inherited access widens the damage of any mistake.
W: A=Per-agent identity: a separate identity lets the auditor trace each action to one agent.
C=Least-privilege IAM: it limits each agent to the resources it needs, shrinking what an error or attack can reach.
X: B=Shared administrator: one account for all agents makes the audit trail useless and grants more access than needed.
D=Logging off: audit needs the logs; the stated approach is auditable records, not removing them.
E=Inherited employee access: it gives agents the creator's full rights instead of purpose-specific permissions.
