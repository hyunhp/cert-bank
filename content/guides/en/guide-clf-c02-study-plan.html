AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02) is the exam many people take first: project managers, sales and finance staff, students, and engineers who are new to AWS. It does not ask you to configure anything. It asks whether you can tell what a service is for, who is responsible for what, and how AWS charges for it. This guide is written for readers who do not build systems every day, and it is organised around the vocabulary that the exam keeps testing.

Study guide · AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02)

The exam

65 questions (50 scored, 15 unscored and unmarked) in 90 minutes; a passing score of 700 on a scale of 100–1,000. Four domains: Cloud Concepts (24%), Security and Compliance (30%), Cloud Technology and Services (34%), and Billing, Pricing, and Support (12%).

Two facts from the 325 CLF-C02 questions we wrote for this site shape how to prepare. First, more than half describe a small business situation rather than ask for a definition, so knowing a service's name is not enough; you need to know when someone would want it. Second, about one question in six has two correct answers. Those questions are where most points are lost, because one right and one wrong choice scores zero.

Beginner tip: open an AWS account on the Free Tier and, before anything else, create a budget alert in AWS Budgets for a small amount such as $5. It takes two minutes, it protects you while you explore, and the Billing domain questions about Budgets, Cost Explorer and alerts will then describe something you have seen.

Domain by domain: what to be able to say in one sentence

Cloud Concepts (24%)

Be able to explain the six advantages of cloud computing (trade fixed expense for variable expense, benefit from economies of scale, stop guessing capacity, increase speed and agility, stop spending money on data centres, go global in minutes), the deployment models (cloud, hybrid, on premises), and the two frameworks AWS uses to talk about good practice:

  • The Well-Architected Framework has six pillars: operational excellence, security, reliability, performance efficiency, cost optimisation and sustainability. Questions describe a goal and ask which pillar it belongs to.
  • The Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF) has six perspectives: business, people, governance (the business side) and platform, security, operations (the technical side). Questions ask which perspective covers, for example, staff training (people) or portfolio management (governance).

Security and Compliance (30%)

The largest single domain. Start with the shared responsibility model: AWS secures the cloud itself, you secure what you put in it, and the line moves with the service. Then learn the security services as a set, because the exam's favourite trick is to offer four of them for one job:

Cloud Technology and Services (34%)

The widest domain, but shallow. For each core service know what it is for and its closest neighbour: EC2 versus Lambda versus containers; S3 versus EBS versus EFS; RDS versus Aurora versus DynamoDB; CloudFront versus Global Accelerator; Regions, Availability Zones and edge locations. Amazon EC2 and Amazon S3 appear in more of our questions than any other service, so start there.

Going deeper: what an Availability Zone actually is

An Availability Zone is not one building. It is one or more data centres with their own power, cooling and networking, close enough to the other zones of the Region for fast, low-latency connections but far enough apart that a fire, flood or power failure in one should not reach the others. That is the whole reason "deploy across two or more AZs" is the standard answer to "keep running if a data centre fails", and why it is not enough for "keep running if the whole Region fails". For that, you need a second Region.

Billing, Pricing, and Support (12%)

Small but precise. Know the pricing models (On-Demand, Savings Plans, Reserved Instances, Spot), the cost tools (Pricing Calculator to estimate before you build, Cost Explorer to analyse what you spent, Budgets to alert, Cost and Usage Report for the full detail) and the support plans. AWS is replacing its plans: since December 2025 the paid plans on sale are Business Support+, Enterprise Support and Unified Operations, and Developer, Business and Enterprise On-Ramp end on January 1, 2027. The CLF-C02 exam guide predates the change, so learn what each tier adds rather than a fixed list:

  • Basic: included for every account. Customer service, documentation and AWS re:Post, the core Trusted Advisor checks, and AWS Health.
  • Developer (ends January 1, 2027): business-hours email access to support engineers.
  • Business (ends January 1, 2027) and its successor Business Support+: 24/7 access to AWS support engineers; the full set of Trusted Advisor checks on Business.
  • Enterprise On-Ramp (ends January 1, 2027): a pool of Technical Account Managers.
  • Enterprise Support: a designated Technical Account Manager and the fastest response for business-critical issues.
  • Unified Operations (new): for mission-critical workloads that need application-specific expertise.

"Needs a designated TAM" points to Enterprise Support; "needs 24/7 access to support engineers at the lowest cost" points to Business (Business Support+ in the new line-up).

Two-answer questions: a method

For a "select two" question, do not look for the two best options. Remove options instead: first anything that is false in itself, then anything that is true but does not answer this question. The two left are usually clear. If three remain, re-read the stem for a word you skipped, typically "most cost-effective" or "managed".

Beginner tip: when two services sound alike, write each one's job as a question, like the security table above ("who did what?" for CloudTrail). Questions are easier to match with a scenario than definitions, because the scenario is usually the question someone is asking.
