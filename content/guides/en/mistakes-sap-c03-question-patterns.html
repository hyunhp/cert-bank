Wrong answers on the AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Professional exam (SAP-C03) are rarely silly. They are usually good designs that solve a slightly different problem from the one in the question. Reading back through the explanations for all 509 SAP-C03 questions in our practice bank, the tempting wrong options keep falling into four families. Each family has a test you can apply before you choose, and knowing which family you tend to fall into is worth more than another hundred questions answered blind.

Why SAP-C03 punishes "almost right"

SAP-C03 replaces SAP-C02 from 17 November 2026: 75 questions in 180 minutes, passing score 720. The questions are long (about 45 words on average in our bank, against about 26 for the AI Business Strategist exam) and 64% are use-case scenarios. Each scenario carries several requirements at once, such as a recovery objective, a compliance rule, a cost limit and "least operational overhead", and three of the four options usually meet most of them. The exam is decided by the one requirement an option misses.

Family 1: fixing the wrong objective

The design addresses a real weakness, just not the one the question measured. Recovery questions are the classic case, because RTO (how long recovery takes) and RPO (how much data is lost) sound alike and have completely different fixes.

Test before choosing: name the number that failed (minutes of downtime, minutes of data, a percentile, a cost line) and reject every option that does not change that number.

Family 2: detecting when the requirement says prevent

Organisation-scale governance is a heavy theme in our SAP-C03 set: AWS Organizations is the most frequently tagged service there, slightly ahead of Amazon S3. The trap is choosing a control that reports a violation when the question demands that the violation never happens.

  • Data residency across the organisation. An AWS Config rule flags buckets created in the wrong Region; an EventBridge rule could delete them. Both act after the data has landed. The answer that prevents creation is an SCP on the OU that denies the actions when aws:RequestedRegion is not approved.
  • Mandatory tags for chargeback. Billing reports and Cost Categories only show what is missing. An SCP makes the tag required at creation; a tag policy keeps its values standard.
  • Guardrails for a public chatbot. Pointing production at a guardrail's working draft means every edit, tested or not, goes live at once, and nobody can show which policy was in force on a given date. Test the draft, publish a numbered version, and move production to it after approval.

The words to watch for are "prevent", "ensure that no", "must never" and "before". If they appear, Config rules, dashboards and alerts are supporting actors at best.

Going deeper: why an SCP holds where a permissions boundary does not

A permissions boundary is attached to one user or role inside an account, so whoever administers that account can create a new role without it or remove it. An SCP is attached to the organisation root, an OU or an account by the management account, and it limits every principal in the affected member accounts, including each account's root user. A member account's administrator cannot detach it or grant around it, because an SCP never grants anything: it sets the maximum, and an explicit deny in it ends the evaluation whatever the account's own policies say. Two limits are worth remembering for the exam. SCPs do not apply to the management account, which is one reason workloads should not run there. And global services may need an exemption in a Region-deny SCP, or users lose access to services that are not tied to one Region.

Family 3: paying for capacity that sits idle

Cost-Optimized Architecture Design is 15% of the exam, and its wrong answers are rarely wasteful in an obvious way. They buy a commitment or a fixed fleet where the scenario describes variable use.

  • Reserved Instances bought for one AZ when the workload has moved to an Auto Scaling group across three. Buying more Zonal RIs adds commitment without fixing the mismatch; changing the scope to Regional lets the discount follow the instances.
  • Non-production environments used part of the day. Savings Plans or bigger instances still pay for the idle hours. Schedule them off.
  • Interruption-tolerant, checkpointed batch jobs. Diversified Spot capacity, not reservations or dedicated hardware.
  • Predictable day and night traffic. Scheduled scaling plus target tracking, not a fleet sized for the peak.

The pattern behind all four: commit only for the steady baseline, and let the variable part follow demand.

Going deeper: what a Reserved Instance actually is

A Reserved Instance is not a server. It is a billing discount that AWS applies, hour by hour, to running instances that match its attributes. A zonal RI matches one instance type in one Availability Zone and, in exchange, also reserves capacity there. A regional RI gives up the capacity reservation but matches usage in any AZ of the Region and, for Linux instances with default tenancy, across sizes in the same family, using a normalisation factor. When an Auto Scaling group moves instances between zones, a zonal RI simply finds nothing to match and the discount goes unused. Standard RIs can have their scope modified, which is why the bank's answer changes the scope rather than buying more or giving up on the commitment.

Family 4: the name fits, the mechanism does not

These options mention the right family of service but miss a property the scenario requires. They are the hardest to spot because the keyword match is so strong.

RequirementNear miss → what meets it

Every Region must accept writesAurora Global Database (one writer Region) → DynamoDB global tables

Test before choosing: for the option you like, say out loud the one property the scenario insists on ("single-tenant", "every Region writes", "static IP") and check that the service has that property, not just a related name.

Beginner tip: for your next two practice sets, log every miss in a table with three columns: the requirement word you missed (for example "prevent" or "5-minute RPO"), the option you chose, and the family it belongs to (1 to 4). After about twenty misses, one or two families will hold most of them. Spend your remaining study time there instead of on new topics.

Where the new SAP-C03 content fits

SAP-C03 adds generative AI and agents (Amazon Bedrock appears in 28 of our 509 questions), DevSecOps, chaos engineering and post-quantum cryptography. None of this needs new instincts. A guardrail on its working draft is a Family 2 mistake; tuning the model when retrieval is broken is Family 4; chaos experiments without stop conditions on customer-facing alarms fail the same "name the risk you are limiting" test as any other design. Learn the new services, but apply the same four tests.
