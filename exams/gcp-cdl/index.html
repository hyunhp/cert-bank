D1 · Digital Transformation with Google Cloud 18%

Business scenarios dominate: a bank, a manufacturer or an online school describes a problem, and you name the cloud concept or Google Cloud strength behind it. Few questions are technical. Precise vocabulary is what counts, since wrong options often use a nearby term (multicloud for hybrid, bandwidth for latency) or blame technology when the real obstacle is people and process.

What to watch for

  • Translate each complaint into a single term before reading the options: delay, transfer capacity, and a mix of on-site and public cloud each have their own name.
  • When technology was bought but daily work did not change, look at culture, processes and data silos before blaming the platform.
  • Link each Google Cloud differentiator to the pain it removes, for example the private global network for consistent speed worldwide, or a data platform where AI work happens next to the data.
Tasks in the official exam guide (2)
  • 1.1 Explain why and how the cloud is revolutionizing businesses
  • 1.2 Describe fundamental cloud concepts

D2 · Exploring Data Transformation with Google Cloud 18%

Most items describe an organization with a data problem (scattered copies, disputed totals, a weak AI pilot, staff busy with database chores) and ask which concept or service addresses it. Answers are about value and responsibility more than configuration. Look for the option that names the idea behind the pain, such as governance or decoupled messaging, rather than one that only adds another tool.

What to watch for

  • When several teams consume the same stream and the producers must not depend on any of them, think of a publish and subscribe service such as Pub/Sub.
  • Remember what moves to Google with managed databases (routine operations and capacity planning) and what stays with the customer, such as governance of the data.
  • If trust, privacy and agreement on numbers are all at stake, one shared set of data standards fits better than several separate technical fixes.
Tasks in the official exam guide (3)

  • 2.2 Determine which Google Cloud data management products are applicable to different business use cases
  • 2.3 Discuss how smart analytics, business intelligence tools, and streaming analytics can add value in different business use cases

D3 · Innovating with Google Cloud Artificial Intelligence 18%

Scenarios ask leaders to choose an AI approach, not to build one. The usual decision is between a pre-trained API, a custom or low-code model, and a managed agent platform, weighed against deadline, staff skills and how much the result must set the business apart. Data quality also appears often, usually as the reason a pilot went wrong.

What to watch for

  • Check three facts in the stem before choosing an approach: how common the task is, how soon it is needed, and whether ML staff exist.
  • Ask whether the problem truly needs learned patterns. Fixed, exact logic belongs in ordinary software, while large-scale predictions from past behaviour suit ML.
  • When a pilot gives poor results, read the description of the data first; missing values, duplicates and mixed units are exactly what a model will learn.
Tasks in the official exam guide (2)
  • 3.1 Describe fundamental AI and ML concepts and how they create business value
  • 3.2 Explain how Google Cloud's AI offerings can create business value

D4 · Modernize Infrastructure and Applications with Google Cloud 18%

This domain checks whether you can match a workload to a migration path, a compute choice or an API approach in business terms. Stems describe an application's state (unused, monolithic, tied to a data center, overloaded by a partner) and offer labels such as retire, refactor or rehost. The best answer follows the deciding phrase in the stem, not the most modern-sounding option.

What to watch for

  • Define each migration label by what actually changes in the workload, from nothing at all to its code and architecture, then match it to the stem's description.
  • Spot VMs are a cost trade-off: they suit work that can restart from checkpoints, and an option that ignores possible reclaiming is incomplete.
  • When one API consumer harms the others and backend code must stay the same, look at policies in the Apigee proxy layer, such as quotas and rate limits.
Tasks in the official exam guide (3)

  • 4.2 Describe the functionality, business use cases, and business value of Google Cloud's infrastructure offerings
  • 4.3 Describe the business value of application programming interfaces (APIs)

D5 · Trust and Security with Google Cloud 18%

Questions here use plain security language. A stem describes an incident, an audit finding or a training session, and you pick the matching term, responsibility split or Google Cloud capability. Options often differ only in which side of the shared responsibility model owns a task, or in whether a tool prevents, detects or records.

What to watch for

  • For shared responsibility, place the service on the scale from IaaS to SaaS; the provider's share grows with management, but data and access remain the customer's job.
  • Match the verb to the tool: recording who did what and when is audit logging, while finding and ranking misconfigurations is posture management in Security Command Center.
  • Least privilege has two parts in these items: minimum permissions for everyone, and extra protection and monitoring for the few privileged accounts.
Tasks in the official exam guide (2)
  • 5.1 Describe fundamental cloud security concepts
  • 5.2 Describe the business value of making Google part of an organization's security team with its defense-in-depth, multilayered approach to cloud security

D6 · Scaling with Google Cloud Operations 10%

Expect cost and reliability scenarios told from a finance or operations view: steady baseline usage, scarce accelerators, rising memory use, a flood of error messages. You decide which pricing model, signal or operations tool fits. Wrong options are usually real tools aimed at a different question, so working out what the team actually wants to know is the main skill.

What to watch for

  • Match the purchase model to the usage shape: commitments for steady, predictable load, Spot capacity for interruptible work, and autoscaling for demand that varies.
  • Know what each of the four golden signals measures, because a stem may show one signal near its limit while the others still look normal.
  • For people, process and technology questions, classify an action by its nature: skills and ownership, a repeatable routine, or tooling for visibility and automation.
Tasks in the official exam guide (2)
  • 6.1 Recognize how Google Cloud supports an organization's ability to control their cloud costs
  • 6.2 Describe the fundamental concepts of modern operations, reliability, and resilience in the cloud
