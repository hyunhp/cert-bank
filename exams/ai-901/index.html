D1 · Identify AI concepts and capabilities 42.5%

This fundamentals domain gives short business cases and asks you to name the concept behind them: a responsible AI principle, an AI workload, a model type or an extraction feature. Few items need product depth. What decides the answer is reading what the system actually produces or what went wrong, then matching that to the closest category rather than a neighbouring one.

What to watch for

  • Classify the workload by its output: a new image made from a prompt is generative AI, while describing or labelling an existing image is computer vision.
  • Learn the responsible AI principles by their typical failure: missing owners and approval records point to accountability, uneven outcomes to fairness.
  • For model choice, weigh accuracy against speed. Multi-step math and planning favour a reasoning model; simple, high-volume language tasks favour a small model.

D2 · Implement AI solutions by using Microsoft Foundry 57.5%

Scenarios here are hands-on but still introductory: trying a service in the Foundry portal, picking a Content Understanding analyzer, connecting an agent to an API, or protecting personal data before a model sees it. Each asks for the most suitable Foundry feature. Correct answers respect a documented limit or prerequisite; distractors promise more than the service can deliver or skip a required step.

What to watch for

  • Playgrounds in the Foundry portal let people test a service on their own data before any code exists. Remember them for early evaluation questions.
  • Be careful with tasks that need exact numbers or positions from images. Vision-enabled models have documented limits, so a different method or a human check may be needed.
  • When data must be cleaned before an agent reads it, a deterministic Azure Language step in the app gives repeatable results that a prompt instruction cannot guarantee.
