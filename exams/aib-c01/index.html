D1 · AI Fundamentals and Literacy 24%

Expect business situations rather than technical ones: a forecast that arrives too late, a team with little time, a calculation that must be exact. Each one asks which kind of AI, if any, suits the need. The right option matches the approach to the problem and the data available, while distractors offer a more ambitious tool than the situation calls for.

What to watch for

  • Ask first whether the rule can be written down. If the result must be exact and repeatable, ordinary software can be a better choice than a learned model.
  • When a model does badly on something new, look at the examples it learned from before blaming the model itself; missing history explains a lot.
  • If a prediction comes too late to act on, the fix lies in what the model is trained to predict and how far ahead, not in faster delivery of the alert.

D2 · AI Strategy and Business Value 28%

This domain puts you in a leadership seat. A list of candidate projects, a stated business goal, a rising bill or a partner dependency sets the scene, and you choose how to prioritize, measure or protect the investment. Strong answers tie AI work to a measurable outcome; weak ones reward novelty, visibility or the sheer number of projects.

What to watch for

  • Rank candidate use cases on two axes at once, the value they bring and how feasible they are, and be wary of options that favor the most ambitious idea.
  • Link each initiative to the metric the goal names. A useful project that moves a different number does not support that goal.
  • Check cost and value claims for what is left out, such as data preparation, staffing or change management, and judge progress against the original business case.

D3 · AI Governance and Responsible AI 24%

Situations here describe something that could go wrong or already has: stale answers, personal data in training material, an agent acting on its own, unfair outcomes for one group. You pick the control or response a responsible organization would apply. Correct options act on the cause; distractors rely on disclaimers, good intentions or blind trust in a single vendor.

What to watch for

  • Before reusing customer records or transcripts, deal with personal and payment data first. Keeping the data internal does not remove privacy duties.
  • For autonomous agents, look for controls that sit outside the agent: action limits, approval thresholds, alerts, a way to pause it and a path back to the manual process.
  • Read the risk level of the use case. In high-stakes decisions that must be explained to the person affected, explainability tends to outweigh small accuracy gains.

D4 · Business Readiness and AI Transformation 24%

Readiness questions describe an organization at some stage of adoption, often with a gap in data, technology, people or governance, and ask what to do next or how to classify the problem. Good answers follow a sensible order: diagnose, prepare, then scale in stages. Options that jump straight to a company-wide rollout, or that wait for perfection, are usually the traps.

What to watch for

  • Name the gap before choosing a fix. Messy records are a data problem and servers that cannot grow are a technology problem, and each needs a different owner.
  • Prefer a limited, monitored release that improves over time to either waiting for a flawless model or launching everywhere at once.
  • When a technically sound system goes unused, suspect change management, such as weak communication with stakeholders, rather than the technology.
