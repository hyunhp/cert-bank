D1 · Configuring access 25%

Access questions describe who can do what in an organization, often after an incident or a failed request, and ask which identity setup or policy fixes it. Where a role is granted and how groups nest usually decide. Strong options grant the narrowest role at the right level of the resource hierarchy; weaker ones widen access at the project or organization level to make an error go away.

What to watch for

  • Trace effective access through the hierarchy and nested groups before changing any binding; a deny policy or organization policy can override an allow.
  • Prefer groups and predefined or custom roles scoped to the resource over primitive roles such as Editor.
  • Workload identity federation and short-lived credentials beat downloaded service account keys whenever the scenario allows them.

D2 · Securing communications and establishing boundary protection 22%

Boundary questions describe traffic between on-premises, VPC networks and Google APIs and ask how to keep it private or contained. The path the traffic actually takes usually decides. Strong options combine the right private access route with a perimeter or firewall rule that matches it; weaker ones open public endpoints or add rules for traffic that never flows that way.

What to watch for

  • VPC Service Controls perimeters stop data leaving supported services; they do not replace firewall rules or IAM.
  • Private Google Access for on-premises hosts depends on DNS and routes for the restricted or private API ranges; intermittent failures often mean only some names resolve to them.
  • Cloud Armor, firewall policies and load balancer settings protect different layers; match the control to where the threat arrives.
Tasks in the official exam guide (3)

  • 2.2 Configure boundary segmentation

D3 · Ensuring data protection 23%

Data protection questions describe sensitive data at rest or on its way to someone else and ask how to find it, mask it or control its keys. What the data is and who must not see it usually decide. Strong options use Sensitive Data Protection or customer-managed keys exactly where the requirement points; weaker ones encrypt or copy everything without addressing the stated exposure.

What to watch for

  • Sensitive Data Protection can discover, classify and de-identify data in Cloud Storage and BigQuery, including images, before data is shared.
  • Customer-managed and external keys add control over key rotation and revocation; know what happens to data when a key is disabled.
  • Data profiling across many projects can alert you when a table becomes more sensitive than before.

D4 · Managing operations 19%

Operations questions describe pipelines, images and running workloads and ask how to keep them secure over time. When a check runs and what it blocks usually decide. Strong options put scanning and policy checks into the pipeline and keep watching what already runs; weaker ones depend on a one-time scan or a person remembering to look.

What to watch for

  • Scan images in the build and stop the push when severity crosses a threshold; Binary Authorization can enforce it at deploy time.
  • Images that are never rebuilt or restarted keep old vulnerabilities: continuous scanning and a rebuild policy matter as much as the first scan.
  • Security Command Center and Cloud Logging findings need an owner and an automated response, not only a dashboard.
Tasks in the official exam guide (2)
  • 4.1 Automate infrastructure and application security
  • 4.2 Configure logging, monitoring, and detection

D5 · Supporting compliance requirements 11%

Compliance questions describe a regulated workload and ask what the customer must still do, or which control proves a requirement. The shared responsibility split usually decides. Strong options name the control that the regulation actually asks for, such as data residency or provider access approval; weaker ones assume the cloud provider covers code and configuration that remain the customer's job.

What to watch for

  • Under the shared responsibility model the customer still owns data classification, access policies and the libraries in their own code.
  • Assured Workloads sets up folders with residency and personnel controls for a compliance regime; it does not make every service compliant by itself.
  • Access Transparency shows provider access after the fact; Access Approval lets you approve or deny it before it happens.
